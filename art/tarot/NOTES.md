# The tarot — notes for the build thread

Madame Sosostris's pack, drawn for the site, and then the rest of the poem's people as a suit of their own. Thirty-five cards and a back. Every card is an SVG with `viewBox="0 0 300 520"`, no width or height, no fonts, no scripts, no rasters. All linework is `currentColor` on a transparent ground, so the page's colour sets the ink and dark mode simply inverts. Each card carries at most one flat accent besides the ink (gold, violet, rust) and the accent stays put in dark mode.

The lettering (EB Garamond for the name, synthesized small caps; Cormorant Garamond light for the numeral) is outlined to paths, so nothing depends on a font loading.

**The numerals are line numbers.** Each card is numbered with the line of the poem where it is dealt or where its figure first appears, in roman capitals, so a card is also an address into the text: XLVII is line 47, "Is your card, the drowned Phoenician Sailor". Two cards share LI because Sosostris deals the man with three staves and the Wheel in the same line. The blank card is LIII ("Which is blank"). The Hanged Man is LV, the line in which she fails to find him. The Sibyl, who is in the epigraph, has no numeral. Line numbers follow the count in `art/STYLE.md` (so the thunder's first DA is 401, the swallow 429, the Prince of Aquitaine 430).

`preview/` holds contact sheets for people to look at (`reading-*.png` for the nine cards of the reading, `pack-*.png` for the other twenty-seven, light and dark); nothing in it is meant for the page.

## Things the page may want to know
- The cards are meant to be shown upright; none is designed to be reversed. `back.svg` is reversible.
- The blank card and the Hanged Man are the reading's two absences and are deliberately the lightest cards; they sit best next to the dense ones. In the rest of the pack the Third and the Swallow are the light ones.
- Ids for the page: the file name without `.svg`. The line to send a reader to is the numeral (Sibyl: the top of the poem). If the page keeps its list of the rest of the pack in poem order, the order of the table above is that order.
- In the dark theme every line inverts to the pale ink, so black masses (the thunder's cloud, Belladonna's mantle, the crowd on the bridge) come out pale on the night ground; that is the same inversion the part plates get and it reads as a white-line engraving. Gold and rust carry in both themes. Violet sits quietly on the night ground (Tiresias's band, the hyacinths, the bats' window); that is the palette's own doing and is meant.
- Line weights go down to 0.22 units. At the site's card sizes (say 160–300 px wide) that is well under a pixel; the browser handles it as grey, which is intended. Below about 120 px wide the hatching starts to close up, so use the cards small only as thumbnails.
- File sizes are 32–166 KB each; the largest are Philomel, the hyacinth girl and Belladonna, all under the 200 KB ceiling in `STYLE.md`.
- Nothing on any card is text except the name and the numeral, and both are paths.

## Sources drawn on
The 1922 text and Eliot's notes (Project Gutenberg #1321). Pamela Colman Smith's 1909 pack for the compositions (the Three of Wands, the Wheel of Fortune, the Hanged Man, the Tower). BLAST and Wyndham Lewis for the blast lines, the cracked ground and the falling towers; Beardsley for the black masses. Leonardo's *Virgin of the Rocks* for Belladonna's grotto. Nerval's *El Desdichado* for the tower's black sun. Wren's St Magnus the Martyr for the nave.
