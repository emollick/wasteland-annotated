# Frontispieces, initials and ornaments — notes for the page

Everything in this folder is drawn by code as SVG, in the manner of a wood engraving: ruled skies, burin-cut rays, crosshatch for the dark, and a few white lines cut out of the black where a Beardsley would have left the paper showing. Nothing is a gradient or a filter. All linework is `currentColor`, so the page's ink colour is the drawing's ink, and dark mode simply turns each plate into a scraperboard (pale line on night). I checked every piece both ways; `contact-sheet.png`, `contact-sheet-dark.png` and `ornaments-sheet.png` are those renders, for looking at rather than for the site.

Line numbers below are the 1922 text as Project Gutenberg has it (eBook 1321), which is what I drew from.

## Things the page should know

- **Ground.** Every file is transparent; the page supplies the paper. There are no `width`/`height` attributes, no scripts, no external references, no raster, no ids at all (so any number can be inlined on one page without collisions).
- **Colour.** Each plate uses ink plus at most one accent, and the accent is always a small flat fill. The accents are written as `fill="var(--violet, #4A3560)"` and `fill="var(--gold, #A8873A)"`. Used as an `<img>` the fallbacks apply and the plate looks exactly as STYLE.md specifies. Inlined, the page can set `--violet` and `--gold` on an ancestor, which matters in dark mode: the site's violet is nearly invisible on night, so the lilacs in Part I and the lightning in Part V vanish unless the page lifts it. `--violet: #9B84B8; --gold: #D2B15A` read well on `#1A1424` when I tried them. Note that as an `<img>` `currentColor` is black, so dark mode needs the SVG inline (or an `<object>` with the colour set inside).
- **Framing.** Plates are `viewBox="0 0 1200 700"`. Everything that carries the scene sits inside x 200–1000, as STYLE.md asks; what lies outside is context that can be lost on a phone without harm (each plate says below what that is). Focal points are listed so the page can align a crop on them.
- **Size.** Largest plate 174 KB, the rest smaller; ornaments are a kilobyte or two. The shared folder stamps a content-credentials `<metadata>` block into each file on write (about 8 KB); it is harmless and can be stripped.
- **Regenerating.** `src/` holds the generators (Python 3 with `fonttools` and `shapely`, plus the two Playwright scripts used for rendering) and the EB Garamond file the initials are outlined from, under its OFL licence. `src/README.md` explains.

## The six plates

### `title.svg` — the Sibyl in her jar (the epigraph)
Petronius, *Satyricon* 48: "I saw with my own eyes the Sibyl at Cumae hanging in a jar, and when the boys said to her, Sibyl, what do you want? she answered, I want to die." A vaulted stone chamber, two doorways into the dark, a shaft of light from a slit high on the left. The ampulla hangs by a cord from the keystone; inside it, shrunk to a handful of dust, the Sibyl sits huddled in her shawl, her face turned to the boys. Three London boys in caps and short trousers stand with their backs to us looking up; the tallest points. The stopper band of the jar is the plate's one gold. Focal point: the jar, x 514–686, y 146–424; the boys stand between x 430 and 830. Nothing essential lies outside the central 800.

### `part-1.svg` — I. The Burial of the Dead
The dead land at noon, "where the sun beats" (22). The sun is seen through the branches of the dead tree that gives no shelter (23); on a flat stone below it the cricket that gives no relief (23). The red rock (25–26) is the crag on the right, with a small figure come in under the shadow of its overhang, and the shadow itself spilling onto the ground. The heap of broken images (22) lies at left: a column drum, a fallen capital, a stone head on its back with one blank eye, shards. Lilacs (2) are the plate's one colour, growing among the rubble, roots in the dry ground, with the cracked earth of "fear in a handful of dust" (30) across the foreground. On the far left horizon, the Unreal City under the brown fog: St Paul's, the squat tower with two turrets of Saint Mary Woolnoth (67), and the crowd flowing over London Bridge, so many (62–63). On the far right, Stetson's garden (71–75): the mound where the corpse was planted, two leaves of it sprouting, the spade still stuck in the ground. Focal point: the tree and sun, x 560–720, y 100–460. Outside the central 800 and safely lost on phones: the city (x < 330) and Stetson's garden (x > 1030).

### `part-2.svg` — II. A Game of Chess
The room of lines 77–110. On the left the Chair like a burnished throne (77), and in it the woman with her back to us, facing the glass, her hair "spread out in fiery points" (108–109), a hand and brush over her crown. Centre, the antique mantel (97): the coloured stone with the carvèd dolphin (96), the sea-wood burning in the grate (94–95), and above it, as though a window gave upon the sylvan scene, the change of Philomel: a wood at dusk with a moon, and the nightingale on a bough, "Jug Jug" (98–103), rising as little ticks from her beak. On the right, the marble dressing table with the glass held up by standards wrought with fruited vines (78–79); one golden Cupidon peeps over the frame, the other hides his eyes behind his wing (80–81); before the glass the sevenbranched candelabra (82), doubled in it; the vials of ivory and coloured glass, unstoppered (86–87); the candle smoke flung up into the laquearia, the coffered ceiling (92–93). In the foreground the chess table with a game half played (137). The fourteen candle flames are the plate's gold. Focal points: the woman at x 250–530, the mantel at x 560–830. The table and glass run to x 1100 and will crop on phones; the woman and the mantel do not.

### `part-3.svg` — III. The Fire Sermon
The Thames at the violet hour (215, 220), the sun's last rays (225) going down behind London Bridge, seen through one of its arches, the crowd and a motor-bus on top. On the far bank the City: St Paul's far off at left, the wharves and warehouses with their cranes, the steeple of Magnus Martyr with its clock and lantern (263–265), and beside it the Monument on Lower Thames Street, whose gilt urn of flames is the plate's gold, a fire that stood above the fishmen at noon long before the Buddha's "Burning burning burning" (308). At the far right, round behind the gashouse (190), the gasholder's iron frame. On the water: the barges drifting with the turning tide, "Red sails / Wide" (267–272), solid black with their seams cut in white; a second and third barge beyond; Elizabeth and Leicester, beating oars, the stern a gilded shell (279–282); a swan for Spenser's "Sweet Thames, run softly" (176, 183–184); the reflections of the steeple and the piers; a slick of oil and tar (266–267). The near bank: reeds and the last fingers of leaf (173–174), the rat creeping through the vegetation dragging its belly (187–188) at bottom left, and at right the fisher on the bank, rod out over the dull canal (189–192). Focal point: the big barge, x 360–560, y 250–480; Magnus Martyr and the Monument at x 690–790. St Paul's and the gasholder lie outside the central 800.

### `part-4.svg` — IV. Death by Water
The sea from below. Along the top, the surface as a wavering line, and on it the ship whose sailor he was, the helmsman at the wheel looking to windward (320), gulls above, the cry he has forgotten (313). Beneath, the whirlpool (318) as an elliptical vortex of spiral lines with the "whispers" of the current flicked along them (315–316), and Phlebas turning in it, head down, limbs loose, a pale body picked at by the current, the pearl where his eye was (48, 125), a jersey's stripes across his chest. The coins spilling from his hand toward the sea bed are the profit and loss (314), and the plate's gold. Fish in the current; bubbles; on the bed an anchor, an amphora and a few bones from older drownings. Focal point: the eye of the whirlpool at (600, 400); the body lies between x 470 and 720. Everything essential is central.

### `part-5.svg` — V. What the Thunder Said
Rock and no water. The sandy road winds up from the foreground into the mountains (331–334); on it the three of the road to Emmaus: two walking, and the third, "gliding wrapt in a brown mantle, hooded" (359–365). The near crag at left is the "dead mountain mouth of carious teeth" (339). On its knoll the empty chapel, no windows, the door swinging (388–389), tumbled graves and singing grass about it (386–387), and on the rooftree the cock, "Co co rico", in a flash of lightning (391–393). The bolt's core is the plate's violet, cut as a bright gap through the black clouds gathered over Himavant (396–397) and the dark ruled sky. Hanging from the cloud at upper left, "upside down in air were towers / Tolling reminiscent bells" (382–383), drawn as a dashed mirage with three bells; on the far ridge below them, the city over the mountains cracking and falling (371–376). Bats with baby faces (379–381) in the sky at right. At bottom right, an exhausted well (384) with its bucket up; at bottom left a dead thorn. Focal points: the chapel and the cock at (760, 390), the bolt from (690, 0) to (766, 340). The inverted towers (x 230–420) and the well (x > 950) are outside the central 800.

## Initials — `initial-1.svg` to `initial-5.svg`
`viewBox="0 0 200 200"`, transparent, ink `currentColor`. The letter is EB Garamond at weight 500 outlined to a path (so it matches the page's type with no font dependency) and sits on a baseline at y 176, cap height about 116, so the five align if set at the same size. Each is the first letter of its part with a small scene from it:

1. **A** — "April is the cruellest month" (1): a lilac sprig up the left leg, florets in violet, dull roots (4) going down from the serifs into cracked ground, a few drops of spring rain.
2. **T** — "The Chair she sat in" (77): the nightingale on the bar singing "Jug Jug" (100–103), a candle against the stem with a gold flame (82), a knight and a pawn at the feet (137).
3. **T** — "The river's tent is broken" (173): the rat creeping along the bar (187), reeds and river at the foot.
4. **P** — "Phlebas the Phoenician" (312): the sea across the lower half, a fish, four gold coins going down (314), a gull above (313).
5. **A** — "After the torchlight red" (322): the cock on the apex, "Co co rico" (391–392), the lightning behind in violet (393), dry grass at the feet (355).

## Stanza ornaments and the tailpiece
All `viewBox="0 0 120 40"`, one line weight (1.1 units), no fills except the ink of a bell, so they can be set at any size and take the page's colour.

- `ornament-1.svg` — a lilac sprig lying along the line, root and all (1–4). For Part I.
- `ornament-2.svg` — the nightingale between two rules, singing (100–103). For Part II.
- `ornament-3.svg` — the river, with a barge's sail on it (267–272). For Part III.
- `ornament-4.svg` — the whirlpool between two wave lines (318). For Part IV.
- `ornament-5.svg` — a flash between two rules (393). For Part V.
- `ornament-plain.svg` — three florets in a triangle, an asterism, for any break where a section's own mark would be too much.
- `tailpiece-shantih.svg` — for the end: still water, three widening rings, and above them the drop that made them, about to be followed by another. The damp gust that brings rain (393–394) arrives at last, and the three rings are the three shantihs (433). Set it centred under the last line with air around it.

## The mark
- `mark.svg` — `viewBox="0 0 200 200"`: lilacs out of the dead land (1–2) in a double-ruled roundel, the sprig rising from three hatched stones over cracked ground, florets in violet. For the masthead, footers, cards.
- `mark-small.svg` — `viewBox="0 0 64 64"`: the same idea reduced to what survives at 16 and 32 pixels: a heavy stem and two leaves in ink over a crack, the panicle as one violet shape. For the favicon and touch icons.
