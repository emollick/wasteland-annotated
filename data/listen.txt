group: pages
title: The fourth printing, 1930: Brigham Young University’s copy
who: Horace Liveright, New York
when: April 1930, from the 1922 setting of type
host: Internet Archive
embed: none
body: The library’s record calls this copy no. 596 of the thousand and cites Gallup A6a, the 1922 edition’s entry in Donald Gallup’s bibliography of Eliot, while also noting a fourth printing. It is not a second scan of the first printing. The title page reads Horace Liveright, the verso “Fourth Printing, April, 1930”, and it has no limitation statement, the note that numbered each copy of the first printing. The pages are the 1922 setting of type, page for page and with the same marginal numbers; “hot water” at line 135 and “Heimat” at line 32, where letters are missing in the Duke copy, print cleanly here. Copies of the first printing exist in two states: in the earlier, line 339 reads “Dead mountain mouth of carious teeth”; in the later the a of “mountain” had dropped out and it reads “mount in”. Duke’s copy is the later state, and this printing still reads “mount in”.
sources: The Waste Land, fourth printing (1930)

body: The typescript and manuscript pages, with Pound’s cuts and comments and notes by Eliot’s wife Vivien, which Eliot sent in October 1922 to John Quinn, the New York lawyer and collector. After Quinn’s death they passed to his sister and then to his niece, who sold them to the New York Public Library’s Berg Collection in 1958; the library announced the purchase in 1968, after Eliot’s death. The library has digitised the pages but shows the images only in the building, so the link here goes to its record, not to the pages. body: Eliot’s note at the head of his notes names “the two volumes Atthis Adonis Osiris”, as the 1922 printing spells it; they are Adonis, Attis, Osiris, Part IV of the third edition of The Golden Bough (1914). This is the book’s first edition of 1906, in one volume. It gives Frazer’s account of the dying and reviving gods, the gardens of Adonis and the images of the god thrown into the sea, first set out in The Golden Bough of 1890 and enlarged here. The other book the headnote names, Jessie Weston’s From Ritual to Romance, is {link:gutenberg-4090-weston|on Project Gutenberg}, and so is {link:gutenberg-3623-frazer-golden-bough|the one-volume Golden Bough} of 1922.
