# Brief: sources for the notes of the annotated Waste Land

You are adding a list of sources to the notes of an annotated edition of Eliot's *The Waste Land* (live at https://the-waste-land.netlify.app). Ethan, the site's owner, asked: "you should cite the scholarly sources you draw on!" The notes were rewritten this afternoon by a scholar and a reader debating every note; the records of those debates say what the scholar checked and against which copy. Your job is to write, for each record in your batch, the sources it draws on, in the form a scholarly edition uses, citing only what was actually consulted or can be verified now.

## The rules

1. **Cite only what is verified.** A citation may go on a record only if one of these holds: (a) the debate record shows the scholar opened that source (a "Checked:" line, a "sources" list, or a URL beside the note); (b) you open it yourself now (Gutenberg, Wikisource, the Latin Library, archive.org open texts, a journal's page); or (c) for a book in copyright that is not open online (Ricks and McCue, Southam, Rainey, the 1971 facsimile, the Letters, Grover Smith, Brooks, Gordon, Crawford, Kenner, Gardner, Matthiessen), a search inside the scanned copy confirms that the book has the point: `python3 /tmp/claude-0/-home-user/41743ec1-7328-5bdc-8190-6efb94baf571/scratchpad/cite/ol.py '"distinctive phrase"' --limit 40` searches inside the scanned books on archive.org (through Open Library) and prints the books, with a snippet, in which the phrase occurs. Add a second term to narrow to one book (`'"superior bank clerk" Stetson'`). Its "leaf" numbers are not printed page numbers; do not turn them into page references. Quote marks inside the query are needed for a phrase.
2. **No guessed page numbers.** Give a page number only when you have seen the page (an open scan, or the printed page number quoted in a source you opened, or a page reference the debate record's scholar gave from a source they opened and named). Otherwise locate by the work's own structure: canto and line, act and scene, chapter and verse, the line a commentary's note is keyed to ("Ricks and McCue, commentary on line 43"; "Southam, Guide, note on line 69"; "Rainey, Annotated Waste Land, note on line 128"), a letter by writer, recipient and date ("Letters, vol. 1, Eliot to his mother, 23 March 1917"), a numbered letter in Paige ("Pound, Letters, ed. Paige, no. 181"). For the facsimile, "Facsimile (1971), p. 47" only if a source you opened gives that page; otherwise "Facsimile (1971)" with the part ("the typescript of Part III").
3. **Where a claim rests on nothing that can be found, do not invent a source.** Leave it uncited on the record and write it in your report: the record, the claim, what you looked for, and which book would settle it. Wikipedia and the site's own pages are not sources; a claim that rests on Wikipedia alone goes in the report as such (a Wikipedia article's own cited source may be checked and cited instead).
4. **What to cite, per record, in this order:** (i) the primary text the note quotes or describes, with its locator; (ii) Eliot's other writings the note draws on (essays, interviews, letters), and documents (the facsimile, Pound's letters, Woolf's diary, the Simancas calendar); (iii) the scholarship the note names or leans on, by name where the note names a critic ("Cleanth Brooks (1939)" needs "Brooks (1939)" in the sources), and, for readings the note gives as "usually taken" or "commentators", the standard commentary where you have confirmed the reading is recorded. Do not cite Eliot's own 1922 notes to the poem (they are on the page beside the note) or the poem itself.
5. **Short forms.** Use the `short:` form from the works list `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/41743ec1-7328-5bdc-8190-6efb94baf571/scratchpad/cite/works.txt`, followed by the locator, and nothing else: "Petronius, Satyricon 48"; "Ovid, Metamorphoses XIV 130–153"; "Dante, Inferno III 55–57"; "Weston, From Ritual to Romance, ch. XIII"; "King James Bible, Ezekiel 2:1"; "Frazer, The Golden Bough (1922), ch. XXXII"; "Brooks (1939)"; "Southam, Guide, note on line 69"; "Ricks and McCue, commentary on line 8"; "Letters, vol. 1, Eliot to Sydney Schiff, 4 November 1921"; "Facsimile (1971), p. 129"; "Pound, Letters, ed. Paige, nos. 181–183"; "Edmund Wilson (1971)". A work not in the list: cite it in the same style and add it to your `new-works` file with `short`, `full` (author, title, edition, place, date, and journal volume and pages where it is an article) and `url` if open; verify its details (Open Library's catalogue https://openlibrary.org/search.json?title=...&author=..., the journal's site, a web search), and mark anything unverified. Titles of works go in <i>…</i> in the `full` form, not in the short form.
6. **The style is the site's:** the plainness of a scholarly editor; no commentary in a citation, no "see also", no "cf.", no "passim", no "et al."; author surnames as the list gives them; en dashes in ranges (130–153); "ch." for chapter, "bk." for book, "p." and "pp." for pages; roman numerals for cantos, books of Ovid, acts (Act IV scene v as "IV.v"), chapters of Weston as she numbers them; arabic for lines, chapters of novels, verses.
7. **Report errors, do not fix them.** If checking a source shows the note is wrong (a misquotation, a wrong chapter, a date), write it in your report with the evidence. Do not edit the note files; nothing in `/mnt/project-files/` is yours to change.
8. **Rights:** quote nothing of Eliot's writing after 1929 (letters, essays, interviews, the drafts) in your report beyond a phrase.

## What a record needs

- A gloss (`glosses.txt`), a drafts record (`drafts.txt`), a library record (`site/sources.txt`), a voice note (`voices.json`), a listening-room record (`listen.txt`): a `sources` list of one to about six citations. A record that only quotes the poem or Eliot's notes, or only describes the recording it plays, may need none; say so in the output with an empty list.
- A "way in" (`ways.txt`): the same, where it makes a claim beyond pointing at a lens or a tool. Many ways need one citation or none.
- Pathway stops are not in this batch.
