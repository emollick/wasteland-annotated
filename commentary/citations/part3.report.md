# Part III, The Fire Sermon: sources report

33 records: 19 glosses, 11 ways in, 3 drafts records. 29 got sources; moorgate, way:3:1, way:3:3 and way:3:9 got none (the last three make no claim beyond the poem, Eliot's notes and the site's tools). Verification: primary texts opened on Wikisource, Gutenberg, the Latin Library, Perseus, archive.org and the Church of England's Prayer Book pages; in-copyright books by search inside the archive.org scans (ol.py); the debate record and round files for what the scholar opened.

## Glosses

### sweet-thames
- Uncited: "usually taken as the girls and young men of the summer, gone with the season". Searched "loitering heirs" with "nymphs", "summer", "departed" in Southam, Rainey, Ricks and McCue; no snippet. Southam's note on lines 179–180 or Ricks and McCue's commentary on line 179 would settle it.
- The refrain is line 18 of the first stanza and closes each stanza; the subtitle "A Spousall Verse" is on the title (Wikisource). No error.

### leman
- All cited. Southam's "leman" as mistress or prostitute was confirmed in the 1968 edition's note on line 182 (studentsguidetos0000sout_w6y8); not seen in the 1994 edition's scan, which works.txt names.
- Dates: Rainey's introduction has Eliot at the Hotel St. Luce, Lausanne, "from 22 November until 2 January"; Eliot's letter to Ottoline Morrell of 30 November 1921 is headed from that hotel; consistent with the note's "late November 1921 to early January 1922". No error.

### at-my-back
- All cited. The 196/197 misnumbering is in the 1922 notes, which are on the page; no citation needed.

### fishing-canal
- Uncited: "The 'White bodies naked on the low damp ground' have been read as the war dead." Searched "White bodies" with "war dead" in the scans; no snippet in any commentary. Ricks and McCue's commentary on line 193, or Southam's note, would settle it.
- Brooks (1939) has "the allusion to the Fisher King commenced in line 189", which carries "usually taken also as the Fisher King".

### mrs-porter
- Uncited: "the soldiers' versions were cruder than this". Southam's note on line 199 (confirmed for the Australian troops and the Cairo brothel-keeper) is the likely place, but the snippet did not show it.
- Bullen's edition heads the passage "Character 3", "Thraso or Polypragmus, The Plush Bee" (archive.org copy); the lines Eliot quotes are there. No error.

### verlaine
- All cited. Kundry's washing of Parsifal's feet is in the Act III stage directions (libretto, opera-arias.com) and Rainey's note on line 202 ("compare with Mrs. Porter"). The other critics who "have often drawn" the parallel were not looked for beyond Rainey.

### tereu
- All cited. Trico's song is in Campaspe V.i (Bond, vol. II, p. 351); Bond discusses Blount's 1632 printing of the songs at vol. II, p. 264.

### eugenides
- Uncited: (1) Smyrna "a centre of the dried-fruit trade" and the currants as samples; (2) the trade sense of "C.i.f. London: documents at sight" (cost, insurance and freight; documents against the sight draft) and that Eliot's expansion "is not the trade usage"; (3) French as the trading language of the Levant, "demotic" as colloquial; (4) the Cannon Street Hotel "formed the front of Cannon Street station" and "was much used for business meetings" (the last rests on Wikipedia). Searched "Smyrna", "currants", "documents at sight", "sight draft", "c.i.f.", "demotic", "Cannon Street Hotel" in the Southam, Rainey and Ricks and McCue scans; only Rainey's note on line 213 (the City Terminus Hotel, renamed the Cannon Street Hotel) and Southam's notes on 209 and 214 surfaced. Southam's notes on lines 209–214 and Ricks and McCue's commentary on 209–214 would settle them.
- Confirmed: Southam on Eugenides "literally well-born" and on the Metropole at Brighton; Rainey on the Metropole; Brooks (1939) on the one eye and the card "shown in profile"; Weston ch. XII on Syrian merchants; Crawford, The Savage and the City, on the "homosexual Eugenides".

### violet-hour
- Uncited: (1) "The dory is the small boat of the New England fishing fleets", and Gloucester "one of their ports" (the Foundation page gives the Gloucester summers only, not the dory); (2) "Stevenson's 'Requiem' ... is also usually cited" (no commentary confirmed as citing it). Ricks and McCue's commentary on line 221 would settle both.
- Error, minor: in Underwoods (1887) the line is "Home is the sailor; home from sea," with a semicolon (Wikisource, Underwoods XXI); the note prints a comma.

### tiresias
- All cited. Pound's "make up yr. mind" is placed on Facsimile p. 47 by Zeligs (Vassar, 2013, fig. 5) and recorded in Ricks and McCue's commentary on line 251; "Perhaps be damned" was seen in the scan of the 1994 reprint on the typescript page before it, whose page number was not seen, so that comment is cited to the typescript of Part III generally. Nine citations, because the note names five texts and four documents or critics.
- "Critics divide": Grover Smith (1956) for Tiresias as "the all-gathering memory"; Kenner (1959) for the "Eliotic pseudo-person" and the note as afterthought.

### typist
- Uncited: (1) the word-glosses ("combinations" a one-piece undergarment, "stays" a corset, "house agent" an estate agent, "carbuncular" pimply); (2) Bradford as the wool town that "stood for fortunes made in the war" and the silk hat as a top hat (Matthiessen only quotes the lines); (3) "Critics have read Tiresias's watching as compassion and as voyeurism" (no critic named; "voyeur" with "Tiresias" and "typist" gave no snippet in any scan). Southam's notes on lines 222–234 and Ricks and McCue's commentary on 231–234 would settle (1) and (2); (3) needs the critics named.
- Confirmed: Ricks and McCue's commentary on lines 222–230 records Pound's "VERSE not interesting enough as verse to warrant so much of it".

### lovely-woman
- Uncited: "The scene has been read as contempt for her and as a kind of mercy" (no critic named or found).
- Discrepancy, not an error: the Gutenberg text of the novel reads "What charm can sooth her melancholy"; the note's quotation has "soothe", the usual modern spelling. The song is in ch. 24; the inn is in ch. 21.

### strand
- Uncited: "Billingsgate fish market stood until 1982" (rests on Wikipedia; Crawford and Southam say only that the market was there). A history of the market, or Ricks and McCue's commentary on line 263, would settle it.
- The LCC pamphlet does not name Phillimore; his chairmanship is cited to Hansard, where Lord Hunsdon says he sat on "the Committee presided over by Lord Phillimore" and, from memory, that it "reported in 1919". The pamphlet's preface dates the commission's appointment to 26 March 1919 and its report to 14 April 1920, so the note's "April 1920" stands. St Magnus at pp. 18–19: "rebuilt by Wren in 1676, the steeple being added in 1705", "one of the most beautiful of all Wren's works", interior "disappointing", "slender Ionic columns". The 1924 redecoration (Martin Travers) is from the parish website.

### thames-daughters
- All cited. Rainey's note on lines 275–276 has the Isle of Dogs as a peninsula and Greenwich Reach past it. The sails and sprit are cited to the Shipping Wonders article (sails "tanned a rich, warm red"; the sprit the heavy spar), marked VERIFY.

### elizabeth-leicester
- Uncited: "usually taken as the Tower of London" for the White towers. Searched "White towers" with "Tower of London" and "Eliot"; nothing in the commentaries. Southam's note on line 289 or Ricks and McCue's commentary would settle it.
- Confirmed: Froude, Everyman vol. I, p. 244 (Quadra's letter, "talk nonsense") and pp. 198–200 (Amy Robsart's death, 8 September 1560); Simancas vol. 1, no. 134, Quadra to the King, 30 June 1561; Southam's note on line 279 sets the passage beside Cleopatra's barge (Antony and Cleopatra II.ii).

### highbury
- Uncited: "Richmond and Kew are on the river above London, where Londoners went boating". Southam's note on line 293 would settle it; not surfaced.
- Confirmed: Purgatorio V 130–136 (Wikisource); Toynbee's "Pia, La" for the early commentators and the uncertainty; Rainey's note on lines 293–294 (Highbury "a drab, middle-class suburb in the north of London").

### moorgate
- No sources. Uncited: Moorgate as a street and district on the north edge of the City, named from a gate in the wall; "the event" as the seduction and "a new start" as his phrase. Southam's note on line 296 exists (its end, "Moorgate station", was seen) but its content was not; Rainey's note on line 296 and Ricks and McCue's commentary would settle it.

### margate
- Error: the dates. Letters, vol. 1, chronology: "15 OCTOBER — Goes to the Albemarle Hotel, Margate ... 12 NOVEMBER — Returns to London"; Rainey, Revisiting, ch. 1: "on 15 October, Eliot left for Margate"; Crawford, Young Eliot: the Albemarle Hotel "from 15 October". The note's "about three weeks, from late October to mid-November" should be about four weeks, from mid-October to 12 November. Gordon, Eliot's Early Years, has "on 22 October, he moved to the Albemarle Hotel", which may be the source of "late October", but she is alone in it.
- Unsettled: the wording of the Schiff letter. The note has "a rough draft of part of part III"; the Historic England entry and the Observer quote "a rough draft of part III"; Rainey quotes "written only some fifty lines" and cites Letters p. 486. The Letters page was not seen; the letter is cited by writer, recipient and date on the strength of Rainey and the listing.
- Uncited: (1) "on three months' leave from Lloyds Bank" (not seen in a snippet; the chronology for October 1921 would give it); (2) "Lyndall Gordon and others have read 'I can connect / Nothing with nothing' as Eliot's own confession" (searched "nothing with nothing" with Gordon's books; no snippet. Gardner's Adamson lecture of 1972 reads it as "the whole human condition", which is a different reading). Gordon's chapter on The Waste Land in Eliot's Early Years would settle (2).

### carthage
- Cited to the Moody volume of 1974 (new work, VERIFY) for "Critics have connected this burning with the refining fire of Purgatorio XXVI": the sentence "the fire which Arnaut Daniel enters extends the teachings of 'The Fire Sermon'" is in the volume, but the essay could not be identified (the lending scan refuses page-level search); A. C. Charity's "The Dantean Recognitions" is the likely one.
- "generally assumed that he met the sermon there": Crawford, Young Eliot, has Eliot's Pali courses with Lanman and Andersen's Pali Reader, which "contains 'The Fire Sermon'"; Kearns (1987) also has the Pali courses of 1912–13 (not cited, to keep the list short). After Strange Gods (1934) has the two years of Sanskrit under Lanman.
- Augustine III.1 and X.34 verbatim in Pusey (Gutenberg); "sartago" in the Latin; Warren § 73, pp. 351–353 (Wikisource). No error.

## Errors found, in one place
1. margate gloss and way:3:7: Eliot went to Margate on 15 October 1921 and returned on 12 November (Letters chronology, Rainey, Crawford), about four weeks, not "about three weeks, from late October to mid-November".
2. margate gloss: the Schiff letter is quoted by the listing and the Observer as "a rough draft of part III", not "part of part III"; the Letters text not seen.
3. violet-hour: Stevenson's line has a semicolon, "Home is the sailor; home from sea,".
4. lovely-woman: the Gutenberg text has "sooth" where the note's quotation has "soothe" (spelling varies by edition).
5. leman: Southam's "leman" note was confirmed in the 1968 edition, not in the 1994 edition works.txt names.
