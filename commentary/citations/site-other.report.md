59 records; 46 given sources (109 citations); 13 need none (they quote only the poem or Eliot's notes, or describe only what they play). Claims left uncited: 9 (listed under the records). Sources checked: the debate records (site.md B/D, debates/site r1–r2 for B and D, part1.md and part2.md voice sections, part3-rounds), the open texts (Gutenberg, Wikisource, the Latin Library, librettoarchive.com, the Church of England's Prayer Book text, archive.org scans and OCR), the host pages (Poetry Archive, PennSound, archive.org metadata, LoC and HathiTrust catalogue records, BBC programme pages, Discogs through its API, App Store, YorkSpace, SoundCloud and YouTube oEmbed, Faber's product pages, Playbill, the Cannes archive, Wilton's archive, Macaulay Library, jeremyirons.net, the NYT of 1987 through the Wayback Machine) and search-inside snippets of the scanned commentaries (ol.py). ## Listening room

- eliot-1933-poetryarchive: all cited. Greet and Hibbitt, Barnard, the aluminium discs and the dates "1933, 1935, and at a later, unknown date" are PennSound's headnote; 1933, "once thought to be 1935", Lacy Rumsey, the five part pages and "the 1946 recording" are the Poetry Archive's. The Norton year is cited to the Norton lectures themselves (The Use of Poetry and the Use of Criticism, "The Charles Eliot Norton Lectures for 1932–1933"), not to a biography. "Forty-four" is arithmetic (born 26 September 1888).
- eliot-1933-faber-soundcloud: all cited. The track's note reads "In a rare recording from 1933" and the three Faber extracts were posted 21 April 2011 (the scholar's reading of SoundCloud's page data); the app followed on 3 June 2011 (App Store version history).
- eliot-1935-pennsound-columbia: all cited; the two durations were measured by the scholar from PennSound's files. Error: the Poetry Archive does not refer readers to Swigg and Camlot "for the date of 1933"; it credits the redating to Lacy Rumsey and names the two books for "those who want to know more about the recording". The eliot-1933-poetryarchive record has this right.
- eliot-later-harperaudio-archive: two claims uncited. (1) "Caedmon issued it first": neither the Harper Audio item nor the Poetry Archive names Caedmon; nothing found by search-inside; a Caedmon catalogue, or Ricks and McCue's note on the recordings, would settle it. (2) "which had started the first internet radio station the year before": rests on Wikipedia and popular histories (Wikipedia's article cites nothing for it); the archive.org collection holding the item is titled "Internet Talk Radio", and the Internet Multicasting Service's own announcement of 1993, or Malamud's account, would settle it. Everything else is in the item's own text (18 January 1994, Internet Town Hall, "8 khz computer sound file", HarperCollins's "limited distribution", "Harper Audio, A Division of HarperCollins Publishers"; the MP3 runs 30 min 57 s) and in Mitgang's NYT piece of 27 August 1987 (Harper & Row "acquired Caedmon this year from Raytheon"), read through the Wayback Machine because nytimes.com refuses requests.

- guinness-argo-full-bookyards: all cited, to the album (Discogs release 2514620, read through the API: Argo PLP 1206/7, 1975, series "British Poets of Our Time", front cover "Recorded in association with The Arts Council of Great Britain and the British Broadcasting Corporation", tracks The Waste Land, B1–B8 with Prufrock at B6 and Journey of the Magi at B8, Four Quartets). ## Drafts

- title-draft: all cited. Gutenberg 883, Book the First, chapter 16: "You mightn't think it, but Sloppy is a beautiful reader of a newspaper. He do the Police in different voices." (Betty Higden; Sloppy the "love-child" who turns the mangle); the facsimile's contents list (search-inside snippet) gives "'He do the Police in Different Voices': Part I The Burial of the Dead 4" and "Part II A Game of Chess 10", and the facsimile-and-transcript section from p. 2; Eliot's headnote on Weston is on the page.
- epigraph-draft: all cited. Conrad, ch. 3 (Gutenberg 219: "he cried out twice, a cry that was no more than a breath: 'The horror! The horror!'"; the note's lower-case "the horror" follows Eliot's typescript, which is fine but worth knowing); Petronius [XLVIII] at the Latin Library; Ovid XIV 130–153 (the Sibyl to Aeneas); Pound's letter is Paige no. 181, Eliot's reply no. 182, Pound's "Do as you like about Conrad; who am I to grudge him his laurel crown" no. 183; Eliot's "somewhat elucidative" is in Letters vol. 1 (search-inside). Error in Paige, not in the note: Paige heads no. 181 "Paris, 24 Saturnus, An 1, (24 December)"; the letter is of 24 January 1922 (Letters vol. 1; "On January 24 Pound wrote", Cambridge Companion, 2015), as the note says.
