text: Eliot's note names the chess game in Middleton's <i>Women Beware Women</i>; the card gives the scene, in which Livia keeps a widow at the board while the Duke seduces her daughter-in-law Bianca. Eliot quoted the exchange in 1927, ending at "I've given thee blind mate twice", and wrote that "The irony could not be improved upon". In the play the widow answers that her "eyes begin to fail". The watcher at the board sees nothing of what matters; the eyes at this line are "lidless", sleepless as the card says.

path: change
line: 99
text: In Ovid the change comes in the middle of a pursuit. Tereus, the "barbarous king", chases Philomel and her sister Procne, his wife, sword drawn, after they have killed his son; they are birds before he reaches them, with the marks of the killing on their breasts (the card gives the story). The poem hangs the finished change on a wall as a picture and keeps the pursuit in the present tense, "still the world pursues". Cleanth Brooks (1939) called Philomela one of the poem's major symbols.
sources: Ovid, Metamorphoses VI 666–674
Brooks (1939)

line: 77
text: The card gives the speech of Antony's officer Enobarbus on Cleopatra's barge. Eliot keeps its shape and changes the things in it: the barge that "Burn'd on the water" is a chair that "Glowed on the marble", and the perfumed sails are her "strange synthetic perfumes". A queen's arrival by water becomes a woman in a room whose grandeur is borrowed too, from Dido's palace in Virgil's <i>Aeneid</i> and Eden in Milton's <i>Paradise Lost</i>. What she says at 111 is read on the pathway on the loves that fail.

line: 115
text: The card gives Rats' Alley as a trench name; Southam's note on the line adds the suggestion that "rats' alley" was soldiers' slang for the trenches, alley being the word for a trench. The line is his unspoken answer to "What are you thinking of?" (the cards at 111 and 115), so the trenches enter the poem as a thought not said aloud. Cleanth Brooks (1939) set the sterile death of rats' alley against the pearls of 125, the drowned man's eyes of Ariel's song (the card at 48).

line: 139
text: The scene is the homecoming from the war as the poem has it. Albert has "been in the army four years", and the card gives "demobbed", demobilised, as the slang of 1919 and 1920 for it. HURRY UP PLEASE ITS TIME is the landlord's closing time, under hours cut in the war (the card at 141) and then fixed by the Licensing Act of August 1921, eight hours a day, ending not later than ten at night, or nine hours and eleven o'clock in London.
