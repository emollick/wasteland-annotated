# BATCH E: the tarot pack's notes, which the site shows when a card is turned (from /mnt/project-files/art/tarot/NOTES.md: the 'what it shows' and 'answers to' columns; the site strips the trailing 'Accent: …' sentence). Plus the thirteen map vignettes' titles (the <title> of each SVG, shown as the picture's name on the map).

## Tarot notes, as the site shows them (id: SHOWS / ANSWERS)
### phoenician-sailor (card name: The Drowned Phoenician Sailor)
SHOWS: A sailor in a striped jersey and bell-bottoms sinking head first through the water, hair streaming with the current, a gull above the surface, a ship's wheel already on the bottom. His eyes are two gold pearls.
ANSWERS: 47–48, and Part IV (312–321). "Those are pearls that were his eyes" is Ariel's song from <i>The Tempest</i>. He is drawn inverted on purpose: he is the Hanged Man the reading cannot find, gone into the water instead.

### belladonna (card name: Belladonna, the Lady of the Rocks)
SHOWS: A woman seated in a grotto of stone, in a black mantle and gown that leave only her face and hands bare; the pupils of her eyes are wide (belladonna drops); her lips are violet; she holds up a sprig of deadly nightshade, leaves, bells and berries. Sea visible through the mouth of the cave.
ANSWERS: 49–50. "The Lady of the Rocks" is Leonardo's <i>Virgin of the Rocks</i> (and Pater's Mona Lisa, older than the rocks among which she sits). Belladonna is the plant, the poison, the cosmetic and "beautiful lady".

### three-staves (card name: The Man with Three Staves)
SHOWS: A man seen from behind on a headland of cracked earth, looking out over water toward a low red sun and two barges. His three staves are a crutch under his arm, a fishing rod held out over the water with its line down, and a staff planted in the ground that has begun to sprout. A dead tree at the left.
ANSWERS: 51, with Eliot's note tying him to the Fisher King (so: a wound to lean on, a line to fish with, a rod of office set in the waste ground). The composition follows Pamela Colman Smith's Three of Wands.

### wheel (card name: The Wheel)
SHOWS: Fortune's wheel drawn as a ship's wheel, hub in gold, the air blasted with lines. The same man in a bowler hat at its four stations: enthroned on top, falling on the right, crushed beneath, climbing on the left.
ANSWERS: 51; the ship's wheel looks ahead to "you who turn the wheel and look to windward" (320). The four stations are the medieval <i>regno, regnavi, sum sine regno, regnabo</i>.

### one-eyed-merchant (card name: The One-Eyed Merchant)
SHOWS: Mr. Eugenides in profile: a profile shows one eye, which is the only kind of one-eyed man a printed card can have. Bowler, astrakhan collar, long overcoat, unshaven, a pocket full of currants and a handful held out in invitation. On his back a large roped bundle whose contents are not shown. A City wall and a doorway behind.
ANSWERS: 52, and 207–214 (the Smyrna merchant, unshaven, currants, the Cannon Street Hotel). Eliot's note: the merchant "melts into" the Phoenician Sailor.

### blank (card name: The Blank Card)
SHOWS: Bare paper inside the frame. Faintly, at 13% ink, the merchant's bundle shows through from the other side of the card, reversed, as if the print on the back had bled: something is there, and cannot be read. The show-through is in a group id="showthrough" so the page can raise its opacity on hover if it likes.
ANSWERS: 52–54: "this card, / Which is blank, is something he carries on his back, / Which I am forbidden to see."

### hanged-man (card name: The Hanged Man)
SHOWS: A dead tree with one long bough for a beam; a rope tied to it, its noose hanging open. The whole air is dense cross-hatching, and where the Hanged Man should hang, inverted, one leg crooked, the paper is left bare in his shape: the card shows the place he is missing from. Round the head that is not there, a gold halo. Far off at the bottom, on a road, two walkers and a hooded third. A green shoot on the dead bough.
ANSWERS: 54–55: "I do not find / The Hanged Man." Eliot's note ties him to Frazer's hanged god and to the hooded figure on the road to Emmaus (360–366), which is why the three walkers are there, and why the bough sprouts.

### crowd-in-a-ring (card name: The Crowd in a Ring)
SHOWS: On a bare plain under a high horizon, a ring of walkers in bowlers and cloche hats, seen from above, going round clockwise; the path is worn into the ground under their feet. The centre of the ring is empty.
ANSWERS: 56, "I see crowds of people, walking round in a ring", and ahead to the crowd flowing over London Bridge (62–65) and Dante's dead.

### back (card name: Back)
SHOWS: The card back: rings of engraved water widening from a single pearl in a central medallion, inside a violet band. It is the same both ways up.
ANSWERS: Death by water, and the current under sea that picks the drowned man's bones. The pearl is the one from the Sailor's eye.

### sibyl (card name: The Sibyl)
SHOWS: A glass jar hung from a cord in a cave of layered rock; inside it, tiny, a crone crouched in profile with her hair down to her knees. Two boys on the cave floor, one pointing up at her.
ANSWERS: The epigraph, from Petronius: the Sibyl of Cumae, who asked for life without asking for youth, hanging in her jar while the boys ask "Sibyl, what do you want?" and she answers "I want to die."

### marie (card name: Marie)
SHOWS: A sled going down a steep white slope, seen from the side: Marie in front, bent forward, both hands on the rail, her scarf streaming back; her cousin behind her, upright, in a cap. Black firs, a hatched sky, far peaks with their sunward faces left as bare snow, and the sled's track behind.
ANSWERS: 13–18, "Marie, / Marie, hold on tight. And down we went. / In the mountains, there you feel free." (The Countess Marie Larisch, the archduke's cousin.)

### hyacinth-girl (card name: The Hyacinth Girl)
SHOWS: A girl standing in a garden with her arms full of hyacinth spikes, hair wet, looking straight out. Round her the hatching of the garden opens into a bare oval: the heart of light. Dark petals falling at the edges.
ANSWERS: 35–41, "You gave me hyacinths first a year ago; / They called me the hyacinth girl" and "Looking into the heart of light, the silence."

### madame-sosostris (card name: Madame Sosostris)
SHOWS: The clairvoyante at her table in a fringed shawl and headscarf, spectacles on, a handkerchief to her nose (the bad cold), one hand dealing a card. On the table five cards face down showing the pack's back, one face up and blank, and a teacup. A curtain behind.
ANSWERS: 43–59, "Madame Sosostris, famous clairvoyante, / Had a bad cold, nevertheless / Is known to be the wisest woman in Europe, / With a wicked pack of cards." The back on her cards is back.svg in miniature.

### crowd-on-london-bridge (card name: The Crowd on London Bridge)
SHOWS: London Bridge from the river side under the brown fog: two rows of bowler-hatted silhouettes flowing over the parapet, the three open arches with the river through them, a barge with a black sail, and above the skyline the tower of St Mary Woolnoth with its clock at nine.
ANSWERS: 60–68, "A crowd flowed over London Bridge, so many" and the dead sound on the final stroke of nine.

### stetson (card name: Stetson)
SHOWS: A man with a spade in a walled garden bed: paling fence, the earth in clods, a low mound with one lilac bud just showing, and a black dog digging at the corner.
ANSWERS: 69–76, "Stetson! / You who were with me in the ships at Mylae! / That corpse you planted last year in your garden, / Has it begun to sprout?" and the Dog, friend to men, who will dig it up again.

### lady-in-the-chair (card name: The Lady in the Chair)
SHOWS: The room of Part II from behind the woman's head: her burnished throne with gold finials, two seven-branched candelabra with gold flames, the vials on the dressing table, and in the tall carved mirror her reflected face with her hair spread out in fiery points.
ANSWERS: 77–110, "The Chair she sat in, like a burnished throne" through "Her hair / Spread out in fiery points / Glowed into words."

### philomel (card name: Philomel)
SHOWS: A picture over an antique mantel, framed as though a window gave upon the sylvan scene: Philomel running to the left, her arms already turning to wings, her head turned back; the barbarous king a black figure behind her, crowned, sword raised; in the tree above, the nightingale singing. The fire-opening dark below the shelf.
ANSWERS: 97–103, "The change of Philomel, by the barbarous king / So rudely forced; yet there the nightingale / Filled all the desert with inviolable voice". Ovid, <i>Metamorphoses</i> vi.

### bad-nerves (card name: My Nerves Are Bad To-night)
SHOWS: A closed room. The woman bolt upright in a high-backed chair, hands gripping its arms, eyes wide, hair beginning to come down; the man standing at the window with his back to her; between them a small table with a chess board; the door shut, with the wind slipping under it across the floor; a rat under her chair.
ANSWERS: 111–138, "My nerves are bad to-night. Yes, bad. Stay with me. / Speak to me. Why do you never speak?", the wind under the door, rats' alley, "And we shall play a game of chess". No accent. The name on the band is the poem's line: <i>My Nerves Are Bad To-night</i>.

### closing-time (card name: Lil, at Closing Time)
SHOWS: A pub at closing time: shelves of bottles, the clock over the bar, the barman calling out, and at a round table two women with their glasses, one of them Lil with her hand to her mouth.
ANSWERS: 139–172, "When Lil's husband got demobbed" to "HURRY UP PLEASE ITS TIME" and "Goonight Bill. Goonight Lou."

### fisher-king (card name: The Fisher King)
SHOWS: A crowned king seated on the bank of the dull canal, in profile, rod out over the water, his thigh bandaged; behind him the gasometer's iron frame and the chimneys; a rat creeping along the bank, a skull and a rib in the grass.
ANSWERS: 187–195, "While I was fishing in the dull canal / On a winter evening round behind the gashouse", and Eliot's note on Weston's <i>From Ritual to Romance</i>.

### eugenides (card name: Mr. Eugenides)
SHOWS: Mr. Eugenides full-face: stout, in a homburg and a heavy coat with an astrakhan collar, unshaven, one hand held out in invitation, the other holding his documents; currants spilling from a coat pocket. Behind him the Cannon Street Hotel's front under the brown fog, a gold clock on it at noon.
ANSWERS: 207–214, "Mr. Eugenides, the Smyrna merchant / Unshaven, with a pocket full of currants". He is the one-eyed merchant of the reading seen from the front.

### tiresias (card name: Tiresias)
SHOWS: Old Tiresias seated, blind, eyes closed, with a beard and wrinkled female breasts, a staff in one hand; a violet band of evening across the horizon behind him, the city's roofline, a stone block, and two coupling snakes at his feet.
ANSWERS: 215–248, "I Tiresias, though blind, throbbing between two lives, / Old man with wrinkled female breasts". The snakes are from Ovid, in Eliot's note.

### typist (card name: The Typist)
SHOWS: The typist's bed-sitter at teatime: the window with her combinations drying on the line, the gramophone with its horn on a table, the divan with stockings and slippers piled on it, tins and a stove, and the typist standing, smoothing her hair with one automatic hand.
ANSWERS: 222–256, "The typist home at teatime, clears her breakfast, lights / Her stove, and lays out food in tins" through "And puts a record on the gramophone."

### magnus-martyr (card name: Magnus Martyr)
SHOWS: The nave of St Magnus the Martyr going back to the east window: two rows of white fluted columns with gold Ionic capitals, a barrel vault above, a chequered floor, the altar under the window. On the step at the bottom right a fishman lounging with a mandoline.
ANSWERS: 257–265, "where the walls / Of Magnus Martyr hold / Inexplicable splendour of Ionian white and gold", and the mandoline whining beside the public bar in Lower Thames Street.

### thames-daughters (card name: The Thames-Daughters)
SHOWS: Three women standing waist-deep in the river, long black hair down, each looking a different way; behind them the far wharves and cranes, three small barges with rust sails; oily slicks on the water.
ANSWERS: 266–306, "The river sweats / Oil and tar / The barges drift / With the turning tide / Red sails / Wide" and the three Thames-daughters' songs.

### elizabeth-and-leicester (card name: Elizabeth and Leicester)
SHOWS: The royal barge going up river in profile, oars beating: the stern a gilded shell, a striped canopy amidships with the Queen (crowned, in a wide ruff) and Leicester (capped, bearded) seated under it, a pennant at the bow. On the far bank the white keep and turrets of the Tower and a church spire with the peal of bells.
ANSWERS: 279–291, "Elizabeth and Leicester / Beating oars / The stern was formed / A gilded shell / Red and gold" and the white towers.

### phlebas (card name: Phlebas the Phoenician)
SHOWS: A nine-turn whirlpool seen from above, the sea's surface at the top; at its centre a skull with the ribs and long bones round it; three small figures at three points of the spiral, a child, a man, an old man; gulls.
ANSWERS: 312–321, "Phlebas the Phoenician, a fortnight dead" through "He passed the stages of his age and youth / Entering the whirlpool."

### road-with-no-water (card name: The Road with No Water)
SHOWS: A sandy road winding up between two masses of rock toward a gap; in the right-hand mass a black cave mouth with broken teeth hanging in it; cracked earth either side of the road; three flat-roofed mudcracked houses with a red face sneering from each door; dry grass; a cicada on a stone; two dry cracks of thunder in the sky and no cloud that will rain.
ANSWERS: 331–359, "Here is no water but only rock", "Dead mountain mouth of carious teeth that cannot spit", "red sullen faces sneer and snarl / From doors of mudcracked houses".

### the-third (card name: The Third Who Walks)
SHOWS: A white road (bare paper) rising to a flat horizon across a grey plain. Two walkers seen from behind in long coats, one with a pack and a stick; beside them, on the far side, a third figure in a hooded mantle whose hem thins away into the road and whose hood is empty. The two cast shadows on the road; the third casts none.
ANSWERS: 360–366, "Who is the third who walks always beside you? / When I count, there are only you and I together", with Eliot's note on Shackleton's party. No accent: the plainest card after the blank.

### falling-towers (card name: The Falling Towers)
SHOWS: Seven towers on a mountain ridge, leaning, cracked, two broken in half, the tallest bursting: blast lines out of the break through the air and violet shards flying. Below the ridge, an endless plain of cracked earth with hooded hordes swarming over it in rows, stumbling.
ANSWERS: 367–377, "Who are those hooded hordes swarming / Over endless plains", "Cracks and reforms and bursts in the violet air / Falling towers / Jerusalem Athens Alexandria / Vienna London / Unreal".

### bats-with-baby-faces (card name: Bats with Baby Faces)
SHOWS: Down the left a blackened wall with four bats crawling head downward on it, wings up, each with a round baby's face; a violet window high in the wall. At the right a woman seated in profile with her long black hair drawn out tight to one hand in strings, a bow in the other, fiddling on them. Three towers hang upside down from the top of the card with bells in their belfries; an exhausted well at the bottom right.
ANSWERS: 378–385, "A woman drew her long black hair out tight / And fiddled whisper music on those strings / And bats with baby faces in the violet light / Whistled, and beat their wings / And crawled head downward down a blackened wall / And upside down in air were towers / Tolling reminiscent bells".

### empty-chapel (card name: The Empty Chapel)
SHOWS: Night among the mountains: a small chapel with no windows, its door swung open on the dark inside, a cock on the rooftree crowing, a flash of lightning down the sky behind it, a faint moon in haze; tumbled gravestones in singing grass, a skull and two bones; the first slant lines of rain at the right edge.
ANSWERS: 386–395, "There is the empty chapel, only the wind's home. / It has no windows, and the door swings, / Dry bones can harm no one. / Only a cock stood on the rooftree / Co co rico co co rico / In a flash of lightning. Then a damp gust / Bringing rain".

### thunder (card name: The Thunder)
SHOWS: One great black cloud filling the top of the card, its underside lobed, and three forks of bare lightning out of it: the thunder's three words. Below, the white peaks of Himavant, the jungle crouched in humps, and Ganga sunken to a thread between wide cracked banks with limp leaves hanging at the edge. On the water a small boat responding to its oar and sail; on the near bank a gold key.
ANSWERS: 396–423, "Ganga was sunken, and the limp leaves / Waited for rain, while the black clouds / Gathered far distant, over Himavant" and DA, DA, DA: the boat is <i>Damyata</i>, the key <i>Dayadhvam</i> ("I have heard the key / Turn in the door once").

### london-bridge-falling (card name: London Bridge Falling Down)
SHOWS: The bridge from the river: five arches, the middle one gone, its roadway tipping into the gap, the parapet broken, stones still in the air and the splash where the arch went in; cracks running into the standing halves; blast lines through the fog; three small figures running from the break.
ANSWERS: 427, "London Bridge is falling down falling down falling down". No accent. Not to be confused with crowd-on-london-bridge.svg (60).

### swallow (card name: The Swallow)
SHOWS: Two swallows against a mostly bare sky, one near and banking with its forked tail and swept wings, its throat rust, one far; below, a reed-bed and water with the reeds' broken reflections.
ANSWERS: 428–429, "Quando fiam uti chelidon — O swallow swallow": the <i>Pervigilium Veneris</i>, where Philomela's sister becomes the swallow, and Tennyson's "O Swallow, Swallow".

### tower (card name: The Tower)
SHOWS: A round keep on a crag over the sea, its top torn off and one side fallen in; a black sun with rays of darkness over the water; one small gold star; at the tower's foot the prince seated on a fallen stone with his lute, gold stars on its belly, looking out to sea.
ANSWERS: 430, "Le Prince d'Aquitaine à la tour abolie", Nerval's <i>El Desdichado</i>: the disinherited prince, the ruined tower, "ma seule étoile est morte", "mon luth constellé / Porte le soleil noir de la Mélancolie". The Tower is also a trump of the real pack, struck by lightning.
