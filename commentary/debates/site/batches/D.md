when: Columbia University, 1933
host: The Poetry Archive
embed: none
body: The earliest recording, cut on aluminium discs at Columbia’s speech laboratory during the year Eliot spent in America. The Archive dates it 1933 and says the date was once thought to be 1935; PennSound, which holds the same Columbia series, still labels it 1935.

when: The Caedmon recording, usually dated 1946; this copy broadcast in 1994
label: later
host: Internet Archive
embed: auto
body: The reading most people know: slower, graver, every line given its full weight, the DA of the thunder like a struck bell. It was issued on record by Caedmon and later by HarperCollins. This copy is a 1994 broadcast by the Internet Multicasting Service, kept by the Internet Archive as an 8 kHz computer sound file, which is how radio reached the internet then, so expect the fidelity of a telephone. The Poetry Archive dates the recording 1946; the item itself gives no year.

id: bl-1922-first-edition-archive-byu
group: pages
title: The fourth printing, 1930: Brigham Young’s copy
who: Horace Liveright, New York
when: April 1930, from the 1922 setting of type
host: Internet Archive
embed: none
body: Not a second scan of the first printing, whatever the catalogue says: the library’s record calls it copy no. 596 of the thousand, but the title page reads Horace Liveright and the verso “Fourth Printing, April, 1930”. The pages are the 1922 setting of type, page for page and with the same marginal numbers; “hot water” at line 135 and “Heimat” at line 32, which lost letters in the Duke copy, print cleanly here. Copies of the first printing exist in two states: in the earlier, line 339 reads “Dead mountain mouth of carious teeth”; in the later the type had shifted on the press and it reads “mount in”. Duke’s copy is the later state, and this printing still reads “mount in”.
