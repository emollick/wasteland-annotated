## The batch as a whole

The site's own voice at present is that of a clever host: every heading is a hook ("Where the poem is standing", "What Pound cut", "The 434th line called 433"), every control is named after a phrase in the poem ("the violet hour", "Sortes", "Tongues", "Echoes", "Let it walk"), and nearly every card ends on a turn ("Part IV obliges", "Some lines are just lines", "No sacrifice, no resurrection, in this deal"). The commonest tell is that last sentence that ties a bow; the next is the colon followed by stacked fragments where a sentence with a verb was wanted. The effect is that I am told how to feel about the thing before I am told what it is, and I could not guess what several buttons did until I pressed them. What the site should sound like is the About page's note on the text, which already has it: name the thing, say what it shows and how to use it, attribute a reading as a reading, and stop. The labels can stay short; the explanations should be plain; nothing needs a twist at the end.
