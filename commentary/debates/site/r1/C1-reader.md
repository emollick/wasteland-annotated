### weston
Problem: Two of the named tells are here: "It does not matter." and "a very good bad book". Frazer is named without a word of who he is. The what-line is a list of things taken, with no verb and no hint of what the book is; "the Syrian merchants" mean nothing to a reader until the note.
Proposed:
what: The book Eliot's note credits with the title and the plan: the Fisher King, the waste land, the Chapel Perilous.
note: Weston (1850–1928) was a scholar of Arthurian romance who came to believe, under the influence of Frazer's <i>Golden Bough</i>, that the Grail stories preserved the remains of an ancient fertility cult: a wounded king, a land gone waste, a cup and a lance that were sexual symbols before they were Christian relics, and a ritual of initiation whose secrets travelled Europe with Syrian merchants. Eliot's note says the book suggested "not only the title, but the plan and a good deal of the incidental symbolism," and in 1956 he said he regretted "having sent so many enquirers off on a wild goose chase after Tarot cards and the Holy Grail." The chapters the poem draws on most are II (The Task of the Hero), IX (The Fisher King) and XIII (The Perilous Chapel) [chapter numbers: fact?].

### frazer
Problem: The what-line is four items with semicolons and no verb. The note's first sentence is long enough that I lost the verb; "the whole question of whether April can bring anything back are Frazer's" is a rhythmic list ending on a reading stated as fact; the Turner sentence is a wink (the reader has to work out for himself that Frazer misread the painting, and that its Sibyl is the poem's Sibyl).
Proposed:
what: The god who dies with the harvest and returns in spring, and the rites behind the planted corpse, the Hanged Man and the drowned Phlebas.
note: Frazer's twelve-volume study of magic and religion, which Eliot's headnote says "has influenced our generation profoundly," argued that the myths of Adonis, Attis and Osiris (and, by implication, Christ) tell one story: a god who dies with the harvest and returns with the spring, helped by ritual, a sacrificed king or an effigy buried or thrown into water. The volumes Eliot names describe the "gardens of Adonis," pots of quick-sprouting seed thrown into the sea, and effigies of Osiris "made of earth and corn," buried so that they would sprout. The corpse planted in the garden at line 71, the Hanged Man of the Tarot pack, whom Eliot's note links to "the Hanged God of Frazer," and Phlebas drowned in Part IV all come from this. Frazer took his title and his opening page from Turner's painting <i>The Golden Bough</i> (1834), which he described as a view of the lake at Nemi; it in fact shows the Sibyl, the prophetess of the poem's epigraph, at Lake Avernus [fact?].
Alternative for the last sentence: cut it. It is a digression either way; it earns its place only if the Sibyl link is made and is right.

### dante-purg26

Proposed:
what: The dedication to Pound, "il miglior fabbro," and line 428, Arnaut stepping back into the refining fire.

### whitman-lilacs

Proposed:
what: Whitman's elegy for Lincoln: lilacs, spring, and the hermit thrush.
note: Whitman's elegy for Lincoln, who was shot in April 1865, brings together the lilac, the evening star and the hermit thrush singing in a swamp. ## The batch as a whole
The stories in these notes are mostly told well: what happens in Ovid, Webster, Luke, Wagner and Dante is clear at one reading, and the facts, where I could judge them, look sound. The trouble is almost always the last sentence, where the note stops telling me about the source and starts telling me what to think of it, in an epigram or a pair of antitheses; most of the twenty-eight end that way, and the six named tells all sit at the end of a note. The second habit is dropping names (Frazer, Bradley, Guinizelli, Kurwenal, Enobarbus, Alberich, Caravaggio, Millais) as if I knew them. A third is the what-line that is a list or an untranslated quotation, which means nothing until the note has been read. Once the flourishes go, most notes need only a sentence changed, and the two passes show what the rest should sound like.
