## Objections

### eliot-1935-pennsound-columbia
Problem: PennSound labels them "The Waste Land, Recorded in 1935, Version 1" and "Version 2" and says nothing about what divides them, but the timings on its page show they are not one transfer: Version 1 runs 24:53 in all and Version 2 28:29, and A Game of Chess alone is 5:23 in one and 7:24 in the other (The Burial of the Dead 4:24 and 5:04; Death by Water 0:40 and 0:44). The Poetry Archive's five files, downloaded and measured (4:18, 4:59, 7:38, 0:40, 5:48), sit closest to Version 1. So the "two complete versions" are two recordings of the poem, and the body should at least say that they differ. (Sources: writing.upenn.edu/pennsound/x/Eliot.php; poetryarchive.org, the five part files.)
Corrected: body: The same Columbia series in two complete versions of different lengths, about twenty-five and twenty-eight minutes, each divided by section, on PennSound’s own page. PennSound holds it with the permission of the Eliot estate, for study and teaching. It gives the date as 1935, though its own headnote says the Columbia recordings were made in 1933, 1935 and later; the Poetry Archive’s redating to 1933 credits Lacy Rumsey and refers readers to Richard Swigg’s <i>Quick, Said the Bird</i> and Jason Camlot’s <i>Phonopoetics</i>.

### eliot-later-harperaudio-archive
Problem: Two facts in the body. "HarperCollins, which bought Caedmon" is out by a name and a date: Harper & Row bought the label in 1987, from Raytheon, which had owned it since 1971, and became HarperCollins in 1990 (Wikipedia, "Caedmon Audio": "Harper & Row (now HarperCollins) purchased the label in 1987"). And the Internet Multicasting Service was an organisation, not a channel: the item's own header reads "Org: Internet Multicasting Service … Channel: Internet Town Hall" (archive.org/details/RTFM-Harp-940218), and Wikipedia credits its founder, Carl Malamud, with "the first Internet radio station", Internet Talk Radio, begun in 1993.
Corrected: body: The later recording, the one issued on record, by Caedmon and then, after Harper & Row (now HarperCollins) bought Caedmon in 1987, as Harper Audio. This copy was put out by Harper Audio on 18 January 1994 through the Internet Multicasting Service, which had started the first internet radio station the year before, on its Internet Town Hall channel, as sound files at 8 kHz, with HarperCollins’s consent to that distribution and no other; the Internet Archive keeps it as a thirty-one-minute MP3. The sound is poor, about the quality of a telephone line. The Poetry Archive dates the recording 1946; the item itself gives no year.

### guinness-argo-full-bookyards
Problem: The album holds more than the body says. Discogs (release 2514620: Argo PLP 1206/7, 1975, two mono LPs, in the series British Poets of Our Time) gives The Waste Land as side A, Four Quartets as the second disc, and side B as Preludes, The Hippopotamus, Sweeney Among the Nightingales, New Hampshire, an excerpt from The Family Reunion, Prufrock, Gus the Theatre Cat and Journey of the Magi; the front cover reads "Recorded in association with The Arts Council of Great Britain and the British Broadcasting Corporation" (the back cover says the British Council instead). "Recorded … for Argo's series" is also more than is known, since Four Quartets was recorded in 1971 (Open Culture, January 2016); what is certain is that the 1975 album belongs to the series. The title's "whole poem" is confirmed: The Waste Land fills a side by itself, and a listener's SoundCloud set of the Argo recording (soundcloud.com/ludifex) has the five sections at 4:49, 5:18, 7:20, 0:35 and 6:20, twenty-four minutes in all.
Corrected: body: Guinness’s readings of The Waste Land and Four Quartets were issued by Argo in its series British Poets of Our Time, made with the Arts Council of Great Britain and the BBC; the double album of 1975 gathers them with eight shorter pieces, Prufrock and Journey of the Magi among them. The copy online is a listener’s upload to YouTube, so it is linked from here and not played on this page.

### shakespearian-rag-york-sheet-music
Problem: Nothing advertises the song as a Follies number. York's cover, read from the scan, carries the title, "Lyric by Gene Buck and Herman Ruby, Music by Dave Stamper" and a photograph of Carrie Reynolds, and York's catalogue record notes no Follies; the Variety advertisement of 19 July 1912 that B. R. McElderry cited (American Quarterly, 1957) listed the song among ten titles as a "big surprise hit" without naming the Follies; and Matthew Guerrieri (Soho the Dog, 10 March 2017) argues from the Variety and New York Times notices that the song was dropped before the 1912 Follies opened and first reached a stage on 5 January 1913. The Follies, which the commentaries repeat from McElderry, must be given as a report, not as the publisher's claim.
Corrected: body: Line 128’s “O O O O that Shakespeherian Rag” adds a syllable to a popular song of 1912, usually said to have been sung in that year’s Ziegfeld Follies; the cover spells it “Shakesperian”. York University in Toronto has the sheet music, scanned with its cover. No recording from the time has turned up in the Library of Congress or the Internet Archive.

### xc715748-hermit-thrush-by-sa
Problem: A small one. CC BY-SA asks for two things, credit and the same licence on whatever is made from the recording; "may be reused with credit" gives only one (creativecommons.org, Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0). I could not reach xeno-canto to confirm the licence itself (see below), so this corrects the wording, not the licence.
Corrected: body: A second recording. Eliot’s note on line 357 speaks of the hermit-thrush’s “water-dripping song”; the bird sings at lines 356 to 358. Licensed CC BY-SA, so this one may be reused with credit and under the same licence.

### frazer-adonis-attis-osiris-1906
Problem: "where Frazer's account … first appeared" is wrong, and the fault is mine from round one. The first edition of The Golden Bough (1890) already has sections on Adonis (p. 278), Attis (p. 296) and Osiris (p. 301) in chapter III, "Killing the God", with the gardens of Adonis and the images thrown into the water (Wikisource, The Golden Bough (1890), contents). The 1906 book enlarged that account into a volume of its own.
Corrected: body: Eliot’s headnote names “the two volumes Adonis, Attis, Osiris”, which are Part IV of the third edition of The Golden Bough (1914). This is the book’s first edition of 1906, in one volume. It gives Frazer’s account of the dying and reviving gods, the gardens of Adonis and the images of the god thrown into the sea, which he had first set out in The Golden Bough of 1890 and enlarged here. Jessie Weston’s From Ritual to Romance is {link:gutenberg-4090-weston|on Project Gutenberg}, and so is {link:gutenberg-3623-frazer-golden-bough|the one-volume Golden Bough} of 1922.
