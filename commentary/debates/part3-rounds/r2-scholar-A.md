# Round 2, scholar, batch A

Three answers first.

thames-daughters (not my batch, but asked): confirm both. The working sails of a Thames sailing barge, the ones left aloft, were dressed with a mixture of red ochre, cod oil, urine and seawater, which gave the flax its rusty red; jibs, stowed below, were left undressed (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thames_sailing_barge). The mainsail is loose-footed, with no boom, and is set on the sprit, a spar about 59 feet long and 10½ inches thick running from the foot of the mast to the peak of the sail; it is the only heavy spar on a barge besides the mast, and when the barge runs to leeward the sail swings out on it. "The heavy spar is the sprit" can stand as fact. "Dressed with red ochre and oil" is exacter than "dressed red-brown", but the sense is the same.

leman quote: confirm. The 1662 psalter reads "By the waters of Babylon we sat down and wept : when we remembered thee, O Sion." (churchofengland.org, Book of Common Prayer psalter, Psalms 136–138, fetched); the American 1928 book has the same words with an asterisk for the pointing (episcopalnet.org/1928bcp/Psalter/Ps137.html, fetched). The quote field as set is the exact Coverdale wording; the Prayer Book spaces the pointing colon, which is typography, not text.

Rainey's Paris dating in leman: not needed. The rewrite says where Eliot was, not where the line was written. It belongs in the drafts record, marked there as Rainey's; I have not seen the facsimile page from here.
