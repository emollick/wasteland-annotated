## day-parliament-of-bees
Object, twice. (1) "may be" is too weak. In Day the words that follow fix the sense: "Actaeon to Diana in the spring, / Where all shall see her naked skin; and there / Actaeons hounds shall their owne Master teare" (Bullen, p. 24); "where" and "there" are a place, and Ovid's Diana bathes at a spring, "fons sonat a dextra tenui perlucidus unda" (III.161). Replace the sentence with: In Day "the spring" is the water Diana bathes in, as in Ovid ("Where all shall see her naked skin"); in Eliot's line it is the season. (2) "The only edition is the quarto of 1641" overstates: Bullen said so in 1881, but the Early English Books microfilms list an issue dated 1643 (archive.org bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_the-parliament-of-bees-_day-john-1574-1640_1643), which I could not examine; the 1697 "Parliament of Bees. A Fable" is a different work. Replace with: The earliest edition that survives is the quarto of 1641; an edition of 1607 was reported by early cataloguers but has never been found, so when Day wrote it is not known.
