# Round 3, reader, batch D: the five altered places only

## ovid-met6
Accept. "Displayed above the mantel" is the poem's own wording at 97–99.

## day-parliament-of-bees
Accept both. "In Day 'the spring' is the water Diana bathes in, as in Ovid ('Where all shall see her naked skin'); in Eliot's line it is the season" is clear and gives its evidence; "The earliest edition that survives is the quarto of 1641; when Day wrote it is not known" is plain.

## lyly-campaspe
Accept. "Whether he wrote them has long been doubted; W. W. Greg argued in 1905 that they are not his" says how old the doubt is and names one doubter, which is what a reader can use.

## buddha-fire-sermon
Alternative. As added, "it is generally assumed he met the sermon there; the series was edited by Charles Lanman, who taught Eliot Sanskrit" hangs the ground after the assumption on a semicolon, so a reader has to work out that the second clause explains the first. Put the ground first and split: "It was the standard English anthology of the Pali texts when Eliot studied Sanskrit and Indian philosophy at Harvard in 1911–13, and the series it appeared in was edited by Charles Lanman, who taught him Sanskrit. It is generally assumed he met the sermon there."
