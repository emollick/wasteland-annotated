# Round 2, reader, batch A

## sweet-thames
Object: "Eliot keeps the refrain (again at 183–184); his nymphs "are departed"." A semicolon stacking a fragment, and the quotation tells me nothing I have not just read; the nymphs as the girls of the summer, which both sides had in round 1, have dropped out, so "their friends" now hangs on nothing. Replace the last two sentences with: "Eliot repeats the refrain at 183–184. His nymphs and "their friends, the loitering heirs of city directors" are usually taken as the girls and young men of the summer, gone with the season, and the litter of summer nights with them."

## leman
Object: "Eliot was at Lausanne, on its shore, from late November 1921 to early January 1922, on sick leave from Lloyds Bank and under the care of Dr Roger Vittoz; Parts IV and V were written there." The doctor's name does not help with the line, and the semicolon tacks a second fact on. Replace with: "Eliot was at Lausanne, on its shore, from late November 1921 to early January 1922, on sick leave from Lloyds Bank and under a doctor's care. Parts IV and V were written there." If the scholar wants the name, keep it, but still break the sentence.

## at-my-back
Object: "in which the speaker urges his mistress to yield" and then "Here what is heard at the speaker's back": two poems' speakers under one name in consecutive sentences. And "the Marvell and Day notes carry each other's numbers, 197 and 196" makes the reader work out which is which. Replace with: "Marvell, "To His Coy Mistress" (published 1681), in which the lover urges his mistress to yield because time is short and the grave is near. Eliot uses the line twice. Here what is heard "at my back" is "the rattle of the bones, and chuckle spread from ear to ear"; at line 196 it is "the sound of horns and motors". Eliot's note gives the source at the second use, line 196. In the 1922 notes it is numbered 197 and the Day note 196, the wrong way round."

## fishing-canal
Object: "He is usually read as also the Fisher King of Weston's <i>From Ritual to Romance</i>, named in Eliot's headnote, whose wound leaves his land barren and who fishes again at 424." Four hangers on one "He", and "named in Eliot's headnote" could be the book or the king. Replace with: "Because Eliot's headnote names Weston's <i>From Ritual to Romance</i> as the poem's source, the man fishing is usually taken also as the Fisher King of the Grail legend, whose wound leaves his land barren. He fishes again at 424." Also "The rat and the bones are Eliot's own cross-reference: his note to "rats' alley" at line 115 reads "Cf. Part III, l. 195"." The rat and bones are not a cross-reference; the note is. Replace with: "Eliot himself links the rat and the bones to line 115: his note to "rats' alley" there reads "Cf. Part III, l. 195"."

## mrs-porter
Object: "In Eliot the horns are motor horns, the hunter is Sweeney, the "Apeneck Sweeney" of his poems of 1918–20, and the bathing is done in soda water." The title promises Mrs Porter and the body never says she stands where Diana stood. Replace with: "In Eliot the horns are motor horns, the hunter is Sweeney, the "Apeneck Sweeney" of his poems of 1918–20, and in Diana's place Mrs Porter and her daughter wash their feet in soda water." Also "The three lines of song are from a bawdy ballad of Australian troops in the First World War, in which Mrs Porter is said to have kept a brothel in Cairo; the soldiers' versions were less printable." "In which ... is said to" reads as if the ballad says it. Replace with: "The three lines of song are from a bawdy ballad sung by Australian troops in the First World War. Its Mrs Porter is said to have kept a brothel in Cairo, and the soldiers' versions were cruder than this."

## eugenides
Object, four sentences.
1. "Smyrna, now İzmir, was the chief port of Asia Minor and a centre of the dried-fruit trade; the pocketful of currants is presumably his samples." Semicolon, and "pocketful ... is ... samples" does not agree. Replace with: "Smyrna, now İzmir, was the chief port of Asia Minor and a centre of the dried-fruit trade. The currants in his pocket are presumably samples."
2. ""C.i.f. London: documents at sight" is trade shorthand: the price covers cost, insurance and freight to London, and the shipping documents are handed over when the sight draft is paid. Eliot's note expands it as "carriage and insurance free", which is not the trade usage; the terms were those of his work at Lloyds Bank." Two colons in one sentence, and the Lloyds fact is tacked on after the slip, where a reader wonders what it is doing; it belongs with the jargon it explains. Replace with: ""C.i.f. London: documents at sight" is trade shorthand of the kind Eliot handled at Lloyds Bank. The price covers cost, insurance and freight to London, and the shipping documents are handed over when the sight draft is paid. Eliot's note expands it as "carriage and insurance free", which is not the trade usage."
3. "French was the trading language of the Levant, and "demotic" marks his as the common, colloquial kind." "Marks his as" took me three readings. Replace with: "French was the trading language of the Levant, and Mr. Eugenides speaks the "demotic", or colloquial, kind."
4. "who "melts into the Phoenician Sailor"; the one eye has been explained by the profile faces on playing cards." Semicolon, and "explained by the faces" reads as if the faces do the explaining. Replace with: "who "melts into the Phoenician Sailor". His one eye is sometimes explained by the playing cards that show a face in profile."
The rest, including the Weston sentence, reads plainly.

## tiresias
First paragraph accepted. Object to two sentences in the second.
1. "That is Eliot's claim, made in the notes he wrote for the book edition of December 1922; the typescript with Tiresias in it had been made a year earlier." "Made" for a typescript, "with Tiresias in it", and the point of the two dates left for the reader to infer. Replace with: "The claim is Eliot's, made in the notes he wrote for the book edition of December 1922, a year after the scene was typed."
2. "Pound, in the margin of the typescript of the scene, told Eliot that Tiresias should "make up yr. mind" whether he knew or not." Knew what? With the placement gone, "whether he knew or not" hangs. Replace with: "Pound, in the margin of the typescript of the scene, objected to a hedging word in what Tiresias says and told Eliot that Tiresias should "make up yr. mind" whether he knew or not."
