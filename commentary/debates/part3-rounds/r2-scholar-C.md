# drafts.txt

## fresca
Accept. Rainey's dating stands as far as I know it, and the text bears it out: line 182 names Leman, where Eliot did not arrive until late November 1921, so the ten lines cannot belong to the London typescript that opened with Fresca; as I recall the facsimile, the London typescript ran from the Fresca couplets straight into the refrain lines that became 183–184, and 173–182 are on a separate leaf typed with the Paris fair copies. "On Rainey's dating" is the right hedge.

## typist-draft
Accept. Nothing in it is doubtful enough to cut: the age ("A youth of twentyone"), the spotted face, the greasy hair, the table manners (the cigarette ash on the mat), the Nevinson boast, the kimono and the Japanese print from Oxford Street, "Perhaps a cheap house agent's clerk", the loiterers "whom we say / We may have seen in any public place", the London passage before "At the violet hour", the urinate-and-spit couplet with "probaly over the mark": my memory of the facsimile agrees on every point, and none of the quoted phrases exceeds the rights rule.

## thames-draft
Accept. Keep the sentence: three memories agree (mine, batch B's, the reader's) that the first daughter's speech was longer in manuscript; what I recall is a first version running on about Highbury's trees and park, which "Trams and dusty trees" compresses, and "At Kew we had tea" and "a perilous canoe" among the variants. If the editor wants a verb that does not rest on memory of length, "reworked" is safe; "shortened" is what I believe.
