### way-1

Verdict: keep

Reads as a model wrote it: no

Problems: none.

Keep: all of it. The Woolf diary line is exactly what I want at the head of the part, it carries its source, and the rest tells me what the listening room has and what to do with it. "Notice where his stresses fell" is an instruction, but this is a list of ways in; instructions are what it is for.

### way-2

Verdict: trim

Reads as a model wrote it: no

Problems:

### way-3

Verdict: trim

Reads as a model wrote it: in places

Problems:
1. "no Hanged Man, no water except the kind that drowns." — the ending is a riddle tied in a bow. The first half is a fact I want. The second half is a phrase built to be quoted, and I had to stop and work out that it means the only thing she says about water is "Fear death by water." Say that.

Keep: "Madame Sosostris deals seven cards and one vision. Turn them over one at a time on the table and read the note on each."

Draft:
HEADING: Lay out the cards
BODY: Madame Sosostris deals seven cards and one vision. Turn them over one at a time on the table and read the note on each. Then ask what she leaves out. She cannot find the Hanged Man, and the only thing she says about water is "Fear death by water."

### way-4

Verdict: rewrite

Reads as a model wrote it: in places

Problems:
1. "London Bridge, King William Street, St Mary Woolnoth, the stroke of nine." — a list standing in for a sentence, and it is the opener, so I meet the manner before I meet the information.
2. "the one Eliot walked to Lloyds Bank" — given flat. I would like to know how it is known that this was his route (he lived in Marylebone; did he come in over the bridge?). For the scholar. If it is an inference, say "the walk a clerk from London Bridge station made every morning" or the like.
3. "read lines 60–68 as a man who has just walked it in fog" — casts me in a part. "With the walk in mind" does the same job without telling me who to be.

Keep: "It is a route of about six hundred yards."

Draft:
HEADING: Walk the commute
BODY: Lines 60–68 follow a route: over London Bridge, up King William Street, past St Mary Woolnoth as its clock strikes nine. It is about six hundred yards, and it is the walk Eliot made to Lloyds Bank. Follow it on the map, then read the lines again with the walk in mind.

### way-6

Verdict: rewrite

Reads as a model wrote it: yes

2. "The Water lens tints every line by what it is made of." — a second metaphor. I had to guess that it means the lens colours lines according to whether they name water or its lack. Say what the lens does (the editor should check what it actually tints).
3. The opening run of eight fragments. I would keep its contents, because it is the one useful thing here: it tells me where water is in seventy-six lines. But as a sentence, not a run.

Draft:
HEADING: Follow the water
BODY: Part I names water, or the lack of it, over and over: spring rain and snow in the first sentence, a shower over the Starnbergersee, a dry stone with no sound of water, a handful of dust, a drowned sailor, and then "Fear death by water." The Water lens tints the lines that name water and the lines that name its absence.

### way-10

Verdict: rewrite

Reads as a model wrote it: yes

Problems:
1. "When were you 'neither living nor dead'? What corpse have you planted in a garden, and have you checked on it? Whose shadow at evening rises to meet you?" — the poem turned into a questionnaire about me. Lines 28–29 and 39–40 are not questions in the poem; the note has made them questions and then says "The section asks them," which it does not. "Have you checked on it?" is arch.
2. "The section asks them; it does not answer." — the poem as a person who withholds.
3. "Neither need you, yet." — a bow, with an inversion for the flourish.

Keep: the heading, and the idea of reading with the questions open, which is a fair way in once the questions are the poem's own.

Draft:
HEADING: Three questions with no answer
BODY: Part I asks questions and answers none of them. What roots clutch and what branches grow out of this stony rubbish (19–20)? Where are you lingering (34, in Wagner's German)? Has the corpse in the garden begun to sprout (72)? One way in is to read the section with those questions open. The section does not answer them, and a first reading does not have to.

### summary

What I threw out most often was the ending. Nearly every note that went wrong went wrong in its last sentence, where a fact gave way to a phrase built to be quoted: "no water except the kind that drowns," "whether it is the weather or the people," "as sound, and as a wall," "Neither need you, yet," "The poem is about things that will not finish," "both are true at once," "The first water is unwelcome," "in a suburb." Next came the colon-and-three or the counted fragment standing in for a sentence: "a bridge, a street, a clock," "Two lines, in quotation marks, and a garden," "a card, a poison, a painting," "with a cold, afraid of the police, right about everything," "frightened and free." Then the poem as a person with a method, a trick or an argument: "The section's weather is its argument," "the poem's method for four hundred lines," "The poem's trick, learned from Baudelaire." Last, and worst for trust, the neat count that turned out wrong against the text: "between two lines of Wagner," "the first thing named after April," "the reading's one instruction."

What works and should stay: the ways in that simply say what a tool shows (way-1, way-2, the first three sentences of way-8); the stops that are a quotation and a source and nothing else (water@42, dead@62, dead@74, all the Cities and Eyes stops); and almost all the card texts, which are written by someone describing a drawing and say what is there. Two card texts carry developer notes ("back.svg," "group id=") that must not reach a reader.

Facts I have queried for the scholar, not changed: that this was Eliot's own walk to the bank (way-4, cities@66, eliot@66); "everything she deals comes true" (first@43, women@43); the king in the lake (eliot@8); the Marie Larisch and hyacinth-garden identifications, which want "usually identified" and a name (card marie, eliot@35); "Ezekiel's valley" against Eliot's citation of Ezekiel 2:1 (dead@23); jar or bottle (women@0).

Pathway intros, which are not in this batch and are proposals only: the Water intro is where Ethan's example lives ("The poem's hydrology... Follow the water and you follow the argument") and should be rewritten in the manner of the brief's own "After" sentence. "Each stop is a place to stand, not an explanation" (A first reading), "Every bird in the poem is a musician, and every song is a complaint" (Birds), "The poem's people cannot see, or see too much" (Eyes), "a map of where he was standing" (Eliot) and "two are queens; one is a bird" (Women) are the same tics at the head of each walk.
