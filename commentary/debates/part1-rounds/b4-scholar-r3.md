# Batch 4, scholar, round 3 (reviewed in three parts, a, b and c; 1 of 3 parts present)

### draft boston
Verdict: not yet (one objection)
Checked: Edmund Wilson's review of the facsimile, NYRB, 18 November 1971 (nybooks.com/articles/1971/11/18/the-first-waste-landii/), re-read for the sequence; and, as a second witness, a 2025 account that paraphrases the draft's episodes in order (thecolumbogame.com, "POETS Day! 'The Waste Land' Lees"). I still could not open the facsimile.
Objections:
1. Wrong: the order of the night. A list of events in one sentence reads as a sequence whatever the intention, and the sequence given — policeman, then Myrtle's — is the reverse of Wilson's, in whom the narrator "goes to a brothel where a woman named Myrtle … tells him he is too drunk", and then: "Leaving the brothel, he is picked up by 'a fly cop,' … but he is rescued by a man he knows, with whom he goes off in a cab". Replace the list with: "drinks at "Tom's place," a show, a call at Myrtle's, where he is refused but given a bed, a bath and breakfast, a brush with a policeman on the way out that a man he knows smooths over, and a walk home at sunrise." — "on the way out" is Wilson's "Leaving the brothel", and fixes the order without a second sentence. No draft wording is quoted.

The order question. Wilson's sequence is: drunk at Tom's place; the brothel, where Myrtle refuses him; "Leaving the brothel", the fly cop; rescue by a man he knows; the cab, in which the cabman and another passenger get out to run a hundred yards on a bet; "so I got out to see the sunrise, and walked home". The show is not in Wilson's summary but is in the draft before Myrtle's (the drum in Row A, the girl who squealed), and the second witness gives the whole run in the same order: Tom's place, the show, the loss of Steve, Myrtle's, the alley and the fly cop, the man who knows the officer, the German Club, the cab and the bet, the sunrise walk. So the gloss on line 1 (Tom's place, the show, Myrtle's at two, the policeman, a man he knows, a cab at sunrise, a walk home) has the order right and needs no correction; the round-two placing of the policeman first was the error, and the wording above puts it back.

One caution, not an objection. Wilson reads the Myrtle passage as the narrator's own; the typescript introduces it with "Then we lost Steve" and sets it in parentheses, and the second witness gives it to Steve. The site follows Wilson, and the wording above keeps his "he". If the editor would rather not decide, "a call at Myrtle's — refused, but given a bed, a bath and breakfast —" says the same without a subject.
