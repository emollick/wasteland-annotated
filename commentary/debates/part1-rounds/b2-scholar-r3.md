### ring

Verdict: satisfied

The gloss is right. In <i>Inferno</i> III Virgil explains the souls in the vestibule as "l'anime triste di coloro / che visser sanza 'nfamia e sanza lodo" (III 35–36), mixed with the angels who were neither rebels nor faithful; the banner and the long train behind it (III 52–57, the lines Eliot's note on 63 cites) are those same souls: Dante recognises among them "colui che fece per viltade il gran rifiuto" (III 59–60), and Virgil calls them "Questi sciaurati, che mai non fur vivi" (III 64). "Without infamy and without praise" is the literal sense of "sanza 'nfamia e sanza lodo", and it matches the note on line 63. Text checked at it.wikisource.org, Canto III.
