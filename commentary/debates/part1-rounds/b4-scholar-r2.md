### voice walker
Verdict: not yet
Objections:
1. Wrong: "Mr. Eugenides (207–211)". The sentence about him runs 209–214, and the walker speaks all of it: "Mr. Eugenides, the Smyrna merchant / … / Asked me in demotic French / To luncheon at the Cannon Street Hotel / Followed by a weekend at the Metropole." Source: the 1922 text; Eliot's own note at 210 (the currants) fixes the numbering, and site numbers agree with Eliot's below 346. Replace with: "Mr. Eugenides (207–214)".
On the lens's span (207–211 walker, 212–214 "eugenides"): wrong, not a reading. Reasons for the other thread:
(a) Syntax. 209–214 is one sentence — subject "Mr. Eugenides" (209), verb "Asked" (212), object "me", the walker. The split at 211/212 puts a subject and its verb in different mouths.
(b) Eugenides has no words in the poem. The invitation is reported, not quoted — an infinitive construction, "Asked me … / To luncheon …" — and the poem pointedly withholds the words themselves: "in demotic French" is the walker's description, and judgement, of speech we never hear. A voice with no words is not a voice.
(c) The poem, and the lens, mark speech where it is given: Sosostris's after "said she", the hyacinth girl's and the cry to Stetson in quotation marks. The lens's own precedent runs the other way from this span: at 15–16 the archduke's quoted words ("He said, Marie, / Marie, hold on tight") stay inside Marie's span. No voice is made for words the poem does quote; none should be made for words it does not.
(d) The most that could be defended as a reading is 213–214 heard as free indirect speech — the walker echoing the invitation's terms — and that is weak, since "Followed by a weekend at the Metropole" is the narrator's own summarising, knowing connective. 212 can never be Eugenides's: it is the walker's verb and the walker's "me". If a eugenides span is kept at all, it is 213–214 at most, labelled as a reading; the clean course is 207–214 to the walker, which is what the note's own description already says ("The 'I' of three passages").
Round one: made — the Strand and Lower Thames Street described as the text has them; the one-voice reading owned ("read here as one voice"). Still needed — 207–214.
