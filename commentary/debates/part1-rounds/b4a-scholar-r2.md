Checked again today where the new text made new claims: the 1922 text (Gutenberg #1321, every-line count) for lines 5–6, 35–41, 69–70, 385, 391, 426; Chaucer GP 9–10 (Skeat, Gutenberg #22120); Crome Yellow (Gutenberg #1999) for "the Fair"; the libretto (Projekt Gutenberg-DE) for the sailor's and shepherd's lines; Eliot's 1922 notes 46, 63, 64 and 424 in work/poem-part1.md and Gutenberg #1321; Brooker, Time Present 101 (2020) for Stravinsky and "Opera"; fr.wikisource for the 1855 printing of "Au Lecteur"; Rainey, Revisiting The Waste Land (2005), ch. 1; the text of "The Death of Saint Narcissus."

### source chaucer
satisfied. Round one asked for four things: drop "first great spring," drop the pilgrimage that "ends" at the Chapel Perilous, say the comparison is not Eliot's, and mark the reading as one. The new text does all four, and "lines 5–6" is right. Minor and optional: "birds singing through the night" is an inference; Chaucer's birds "maken melodye" and "slepen al the nyght with open ye" (GP 9–10). "Birds that sleep all night with open eye," or simply "birds singing," would be closer.

### source death-of-st-narcissus

### source huxley-crome-yellow
not yet.
Round one made: Eliot's letter to Grover Smith (10 March 1952, "almost certain," not conscious of it) is now in, with Smith named. Still needed:
1. "the village fête": Huxley's word is "the Fair," the annual charity fair in the park at Crome for the hospital (Gutenberg #1999, ch. XXV, "the Fair has become an institution"; Scogan's booth is at it, ch. XXVII). Replace "to tell fortunes at the village fête" with "to tell fortunes at the charity fair in the park".
2. Missing: the chronological difficulty, which "the accepted source" hides. Lawrence Rainey (Revisiting The Waste Land, 2005, ch. 1) dates the typescript of Parts I and II to 9–22 May 1921 from the paper and typewriter of Eliot's letters of those weeks, and notes that Huxley finished the novel only in August 1921; on that dating Eliot could not have read the fair scene first, and Rainey suggests the name was Eliot's own. Replace "is the accepted source of Madame Sosostris" with "is the source usually given for Madame Sosostris", and add after the sentence on the 1952 letter: "Lawrence Rainey (<i>Revisiting The Waste Land</i>, 2005) has doubted it: he dates the typescript of Parts I and II to May 1921 from its paper and typewriter, before Huxley had finished the novel, and suggests the name was Eliot's own."
Optional: "an old gypsy woman" is a fair gloss of Huxley's "the Bohemian Hag of Frith's Derby Day." Scogan reads palms, so the "wicked pack of cards" is Eliot's; four words would say so.
