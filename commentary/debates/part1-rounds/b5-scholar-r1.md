### way-3
Verdict: correct
Checked: lines 46–56 of the 1922 text. Dealt: the drowned Phoenician Sailor, Belladonna, the man with three staves, the Wheel, the one-eyed merchant, and the blank card — six. The Hanged Man is looked for and not found ("I do not find / The Hanged Man"); "I see crowds of people" is the vision. The site's own Sosostris card shows six cards on her table (five face down, one blank face up), and the cards tool itself has six dealt cards plus the missing Hanged Man plus the vision.
Substance:
1. "Madame Sosostris deals seven cards and one vision" — she deals six. Seven is right only if it counts the Hanged Man, which the same paragraph says she leaves out. This is the kind of tidy count Ethan flagged, and it is wrong.
Draft (body): Madame Sosostris turns up six cards, fails to find a seventh, and reports one vision. Turn them over one at a time on the table and read the note on each. Then ask what the reading leaves out: no Hanged Man, and no water except the kind that drowns.

### way-4
Verdict: correct
Checked: Rennie's London Bridge (the bridge of 1922, opened 1831): 928 ft, five stone arches (Wikipedia, "London Bridge"). St Mary Woolnoth stands at the corner of Lombard Street and King William Street (Wikipedia, "St Mary Woolnoth"; coordinates 51.5128, −0.0881). Measured from those positions: bridge ≈ 310 yards; north end of the bridge up King William Street to the church ≈ 580 yards; the whole named route, Southwark end of the bridge to the church, ≈ 850–900 yards.
Substance:
1. "a route of about six hundred yards" — six hundred yards fits only the stretch from the north end of the bridge to the church. The route as listed ("London Bridge, King William Street, St Mary Woolnoth") is about half a mile. Say "about half a mile", or start the route at the foot of the bridge.
2. "the one Eliot walked to Lloyds Bank" — stated as fact, and I can find no evidence for it. In 1922 Eliot lived at 9 Clarence Gate Gardens, Marylebone, north-west of the City; nothing I can reach documents a daily walk over London Bridge. The route in the poem is the commuters' way into the City from London Bridge station; what Eliot's own note (to 68) claims is only that the dead sound at nine was "a phenomenon which I have often noticed" — his bank stood at the end of the route. Guides and walking tours assert the commute; the scholarly annotations (Southam, Ricks and McCue) do not.
Draft (body): London Bridge, King William Street, St Mary Woolnoth, the stroke of nine. About half a mile, the way thousands walked into the City from London Bridge station every morning; Eliot's bank stood at the end of it, and his note to line 68 calls the dead sound at nine "a phenomenon which I have often noticed". Follow it on the map, then read lines 60–68 as someone who has just walked it in fog.

### summary
The corrections that matter most, in order:
1. way-3 (and anything else that repeats it): Sosostris deals six cards, not seven — the Hanged Man she cannot find and the crowds are neither of them dealt. The site's own card drawing agrees (six on the table).
2. way-4 / cities@66 / eliot@66: the commute. "The one Eliot walked to Lloyds Bank" and "Eliot's route to work" are undocumented — he lived in Marylebone; the route is the London Bridge commuters', and his own note claims only that he "often noticed" the nine o'clock stroke. Also "about six hundred yards" undercounts the named route (bridge included it is about half a mile), and "Lloyds Bank, Lombard Street, across the road from the church" plus "the clock struck nine as he arrived" assert more than the sources do.
3. dead@2: the lilacs, not the dead land, are the first thing named after April.
