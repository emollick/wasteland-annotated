### draft boston
Not yet.
1. “a policeman who picks him up and a man he knows who gets him off” — both idioms have more than one meaning: a policeman can pick a drunk up off the pavement, and a friend can get a man off a charge or off the street. Replace with: “a policeman who takes him in and a man he knows who gets him released”. If that is not what the draft shows, the scholar’s account of the facts governs, but the idioms should still go.
2. “Valerie Eliot’s facsimile (1971) attributes the stroke to Eliot, and Pound’s annotations begin only after it; whether Pound read the lines is not recorded.” — “the stroke”, coming after “struck through”, reads as a different word, and “after it” has nothing firm to hold on to. Replace with: “Valerie Eliot’s facsimile (1971) attributes the cancellation to Eliot, and Pound’s annotations begin only after the cancelled lines; whether Pound read them is not recorded.”
3. “Pound’s comments on the rest of Part I are few: he queried a few words and let the section stand.” — “few” twice in one sentence. Replace with: “Pound wrote little on the rest of Part I: he queried a few words and let the section stand.”

### summary
The other thread’s rewrite passes almost everywhere: 21 of 35 items satisfied outright, and of the 14 not yet, 11 have a single objection. Nothing left reads as a model’s flourish; the bows, the counts and the antitheses are gone, and readings are marked as readings. What remains is sentence length (tristan, inf3, tempest, frazer, conrad, title-draft), two opening fragments (narcissus, frontiers), a few phrases nobody says across a table (“in its stead”, “has been cited for”, “the office crowd”), and the poem made a person once (“wants”, in whitman). The voice legend is done. Draft boston needs three changes, all wording and none touching the facts the editor settled.
