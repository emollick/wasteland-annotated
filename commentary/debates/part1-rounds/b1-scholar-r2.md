### epigraph
Verdict: not yet
1. "The Petronius epigraph first appeared in the Boni & Liveright edition of December 1922; the <i>Criterion</i> printing of October 1922 had none." The first half is false, and it answers the bracketed question: the Dial printing of November 1922 carries the Petronius epigraph. I checked the issue itself (archive.org, sim_dial_1922-11_73): the epigraph stands under "THE WASTE LAND / BY T. S. ELIOT," before "THE BURIAL OF THE DEAD," and a footnote on the first page reads "Copyright 1922 by T. S. Eliot. An edition of The Waste Land with annotations by Mr Eliot will presently be issued by Boni & Liveright—The Editors." So the first appearance of the epigraph in print is the Dial (its November issue was on sale before the Boni & Liveright book of December). The second half, that the Criterion had no epigraph, I could not verify: my round-one source for the whole sentence (the King's College Cambridge centenary page, which says the Boni & Liveright edition carried the epigraph "for the first time") is disproved on this very point by the Dial scan, and the Criterion pages themselves are out of reach today. The claim should not stand on that source. Replace the sentence and the bracketed question with wording that claims only what is checked:
"Neither the <i>Criterion</i> printing of October 1922 nor the <i>Dial</i>'s of November carried Eliot's notes, but the <i>Dial</i> printed the epigraph, with a note from the editors that an edition “with annotations by Mr Eliot” was coming from Boni & Liveright. The book followed in December 1922."
(The no-notes claim for both periodical printings is uncontested — the Dial's own footnote presumes it, and every account of the publication history states it. If the editor wants the stronger claim that the Criterion also lacked the epigraph, it needs someone with the Criterion's page 50 or Gallup's bibliography in hand; I could reach neither.)

### starnbergersee
Verdict: not yet
1. "her aunt the Empress Elisabeth grew up at Possenhofen on its shore": overstated. Elisabeth was born in Munich, and the family wintered at the Herzog-Max-Palais there; Possenhofen was the summer house ("The family's homes were the Herzog-Max-Palais in Munich during winter and Possenhofen Castle in the summer months" — Wikipedia, Empress Elisabeth of Austria, and the standard biographies). Replace with:
"her aunt the Empress Elisabeth spent her childhood summers at Possenhofen on its shore."
2. "In the typescript the lake was the Königssee, in the far south-east of Bavaria; Eliot changed it." A draft fact with no source, where the parallel notes (boston-cut, marie, red-rock) all carry the facsimile. Replace with:
"In the typescript the lake was the Königssee, in the far south-east of Bavaria; Eliot changed it (the typescript is reproduced in the 1971 facsimile)."
