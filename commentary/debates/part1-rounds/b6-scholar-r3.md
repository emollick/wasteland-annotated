### card-note blank
Verdict: satisfied
Checked: Brooks (1947 reprint): "The Syrian merchants, we learn from Miss Weston's book, were, with slaves and soldiers, the principal carriers of the mysteries which lie at the core of the Grail legends... What he carries on his back and what the fortune-teller was forbidden to see is evidently the knowledge of the mysteries (although Mr Eugenides himself is hardly likely to be more aware of it than Madame Sosostris is aware of the importance of her function)." "Following her, Cleanth Brooks (1939) read this merchant as carrying a mystery he knows nothing of" is a fair statement of that.

### card-note hanged-man
Verdict: satisfied
Checked: the hood sentence is verbatim in the 1947 reprint: "That he is hooded accounts for Madame Sosostris' inability to see him" (the possessive as printed). For the record, Brooks goes straight on: "or rather, here again the palaver of the modern fortune-teller is turned to new and important account by the poet's shifting the matter into a new and serious context" — so the hood is his explanation, offered and then reframed rather than withdrawn. The note quotes him exactly and claims no more than he says; the absence-reading is rightly given to readers since him.
