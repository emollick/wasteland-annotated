### draft boston
Verdict: trim
Reads as a model wrote it: in places
Problems:
1. "What remains begins already tired" — the tell named in the common brief ("a poem that starts already exhausted"). Cut.
2. ""April is the cruellest month" is the second first line the poem had" — a trick of phrase made to be quoted. The fact is simply that it was not the first line of the draft.
3. TITLE: "Before "April": a night in Boston" stages the record. "The cancelled opening: a night out in Boston" says what it is.
Keep: the first sentence (the list after the colon is what was in the passage, which is what I want to know); "The whole passage is struck through in pencil, apparently by Eliot before Pound saw it."; the last sentence.
Draft:
TITLE: The cancelled opening: a night out in Boston
BODY: The typescript of Part I opens with fifty-four lines of loose, chatty verse about a night out with the boys in Boston: drinks at "Tom's place," a burlesque show, a girl, a run from a policeman, a walk home at sunrise. The whole passage is struck through in pencil, apparently by Eliot before Pound saw it. "April is the cruellest month" was not the poem's first line until it was cut. Pound's comments on the rest of Part I are light: he queried a few words and let the section stand.

### summary
What I threw out most often was the last sentence: the bow that sums the note up in a line made to be quoted (ecclesiastes, ezekiel, narcissus, whitman, petronius, weston, dickens, frontiers, tennyson, the Boston and epigraph drafts, the lover's legend). Under it the notes are mostly sound. Next came readings stated as what the poem is or does ("are these", "never allows the change", "are Frazer's", "still answers to it", "the poem's one admission of feeling"); I marked them as readings and pointed them at line numbers. Then fragments used as openers (chaucer, inf4, webster, dickens), balanced pairs ("a ship setting out and a ship not arriving", "beautifully and falsely", "a dying man's verdict ... an immortal's wish"), and instructions to the reader (weston, conrad, frontiers). Modern shorthand ("commuters", "office crowd", "gasworks", "in drag") I asked to have replaced with the poem's or the source's own words.

What works: the source pages are best where they say where the passage sits in its source and who is speaking (tristan, inf3, webster, huxley, purg26). That is what a reader opens them for, and purg26 needs nothing. Two notes forget the line they are filed under: tennyson never mentions the handful of dust, and webster never says that the wolf became a Dog. The voice legends are mostly plain and should be left alone; three want a word changed and one (lover) wants its note replaced. Verdicts: 10 keep, 14 trim, 11 rewrite, 0 cut.
