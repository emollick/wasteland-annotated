# Editor's first drafts of the ways, stops and drafts record, written before the round-one reports (to be revised against them)

### way-1
HEADING: Hear it before you read it
BODY: Virginia Woolf, who heard Eliot read the poem after dinner in June 1922, wrote in her diary that "he sang it & chanted it rhythmed it." His own recordings are in the listening room. Read the seventy-six lines after hearing him, and notice where his stresses fall.

### way-2
HEADING: Count the speakers
BODY: Switch on Voices and the section divides among at least nine speakers: the poem's own voice, Marie, a stranger in a café, a prophet, a sailor in Wagner, a girl with hyacinths, someone who could not speak, a clairvoyante, a commuter. The typescript's title was "He Do the Police in Different Voices." Several of them are quoting someone else.

### way-3
HEADING: Lay out the cards
BODY: Madame Sosostris deals six cards, fails to find a seventh, and sees a crowd. Turn them over one at a time and read the note on each.

### way-4
HEADING: Walk the commute
BODY: London Bridge, King William Street, St Mary Woolnoth, the stroke of nine: about six hundred yards, the route Eliot walked to Lloyds Bank. Follow it on the map, then read lines 60 to 68.

### way-5
HEADING: Put Chaucer beside it
BODY: Read the first twelve lines of the General Prologue, then Eliot's first seven. Same month, same rain, same roots.

### way-6
HEADING: Follow the water
BODY: Spring rain, forgetful snow, a shower of rain, dry stone, no sound of water, a handful of dust, a drowned sailor, "Fear death by water." The Water lens tints each line that names water, drought or fire.

### way-7
HEADING: Meet the dead
BODY: Dante's crowd at the gate of Hell, Limbo's sighs, Webster's dirge, the corpse in the garden, Frazer's buried god. The pathway "The dead" stops at each with the source passage open.

### way-8
HEADING: Read what was cut
BODY: In the typescript the section did not begin with April. Fifty-four lines about a night out in Boston came first and were struck out. The Drafts lens marks where the cut fell.

### way-9
HEADING: Leave the German alone
BODY: Five lines of Wagner, one sentence overheard in a café and a line of Baudelaire are left untranslated. Switch on Tongues for the translations, then switch it off and read the section as a reader of 1922 without German or French would have read it.

### way-10
HEADING: Three questions the section asks
BODY: When were you "neither living nor dead"? What corpse have you planted in a garden, and has it begun to sprout? Whose shadow at evening rises to meet you? The section asks them and does not answer.

### first@19

### first@35
Between two quotations from Wagner, a speaker remembers a garden and being unable to speak. The note on line 36 gives what is known about the garden.

### first@43
A fortune-teller with a cold. The cards she deals return later in the poem, and Eliot's note on line 46 says where. She does not find the Hanged Man.

### first@60

### draft boston
TITLE: Before "April": a night in Boston
BODY:
The typescript of Part I opens with fifty-four lines in a loose, talkative verse about a night out in Boston: drinks at "Tom's place", a burlesque show, a girl, a run from a policeman, a walk home at sunrise. The passage is struck through in pencil, and the cancellation is generally taken to be Eliot's own, made before Pound saw the typescript. "April is the cruellest month" was the poem's second first line. Pound's marks on the rest of Part I are few; he queried some words and let the section stand.
