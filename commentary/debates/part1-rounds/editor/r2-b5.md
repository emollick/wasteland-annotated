# Editor, after round two: batch 5 (ways in, stops, artist's card notes)

Only the items changed after round two are given here; everything else stands as in r1-b5.md, both sides having said so. Each item: the objections, the decision, the draft for round three, the last.

### way-2
Reader: "Wagner's sailor and his shepherd" reads as the sailor's shepherd. Taken.
TITLE: Count the speakers
BODY: Switch on Voices and the section splits into ten: the poem's own voice, Marie, a stranger in a café, a prophet, a sailor and a shepherd from Wagner, a girl with hyacinths, someone who could not speak, a clairvoyante, a commuter. The typescript of this section was headed "He Do the Police in Different Voices", a phrase from Dickens. For each voice, ask whether it is quoting someone else.

### way-3
Reader: the seventh card said twice. Scholar: "the only thing she says about water" is a false count, since her first card is a drowned sailor. Both taken; the original's "no water except the kind that drowns" comes back as a sentence.
TITLE: Lay out the cards
BODY: Madame Sosostris turns up six cards, fails to find a seventh, and reports one vision. Turn them over one at a time on the table and read the note on each. Then ask what she leaves out. The seventh card, the one she cannot find, is the Hanged Man, and the only water in her reading is the kind that drowns: a drowned sailor, and "Fear death by water."

### way-4
Reader: the three-clause sentence split. Scholar: the poem says "up the hill and down King William Street", so "up King William Street" contradicts it; quote the line. Both taken.
TITLE: Walk the commute
BODY: Lines 60–68 follow a route: over London Bridge, "up the hill and down King William Street", and past St Mary Woolnoth as its clock strikes nine. It is about half a mile, the way into the City that thousands of clerks walked every morning from London Bridge station. Eliot's bank stood at the end of it, and his note on line 68 calls the dead sound on the final stroke "a phenomenon which I have often noticed". Follow it on the map, then read the lines again with the walk in mind.

### way-6
Reader: the closing tag ("wet ... stone") cut. Scholar: the snow is in the second sentence, not the first. Both taken.
TITLE: Follow the water
BODY: Part I names water, or the lack of it, again and again: spring rain and forgetful snow in the first seven lines, a shower over the Starnbergersee, a dry stone with no sound of water, a handful of dust, a drowned sailor, and then "Fear death by water." The Water lens tints the lines that name water and the lines that name its absence.

### water@55
Reader: "the rite" has not been mentioned. Taken, with Brooks's point put in his own terms (the scholar verified his words: she warns against death by water "not realizing ... that the way into life may be by death itself").
"Fear death by water." Madame Sosostris warns against water. In the Grail legend that Eliot's headnote points to, water is what the waste land lacks and needs, and Cleanth Brooks (1939) read her warning as a warning against the one death that might bring life. Part IV of the poem is called Death by Water.

### dead@2
Scholar: the section's title names the dead before line 1, so "the first thing the poem calls dead" is another wrong count. Taken.
"Lilacs out of the dead land." The poem's second line already calls the land dead.

### cities@8
Reader: one spelling of the lake across the site. Taken.
The Starnbergersee and the Hofgarten: Munich, where Eliot spent the summer of 1911.

### eliot@0
Reader: "Paris" twice in nine words; the dedication sentence split. Both taken.
Paris, January 1922. Eliot stopped there on his way back from Lausanne, and Pound went through the typescript with him, cutting it hard. The dedication came later. In 1923 Eliot wrote "il miglior fabbro" in Pound's copy, and from 1925 it was printed with the poem. The words are Dante's, for the poet Arnaut Daniel, and mean the better craftsman.

### card sibyl
Reader: "the bargain behind her state is Ovid's" made the bargain his. Taken.
ANSWERS: The epigraph, from Petronius: the Sibyl of Cumae hangs in her jar while the boys ask "Sibyl, what do you want?" and she answers "I want to die." The bargain that brought her to this is told by Ovid: as many years as the grains of dust in her hand, and no youth to live them in.

### card three-staves
Reader (a new objection to an item marked unchanged): the "so:" triad; one wording for this and the tool's DECK sentence. Taken.
ANSWERS: 51. Eliot's note ties him to the Fisher King, so his three staves are a crutch for the king's wound, a fishing rod with its line down, and a staff of office planted in the waste ground. The composition follows Pamela Colman Smith's Three of Wands in the Rider pack.

### card wheel
Reader (a new objection to an item marked unchanged): the Latin stations need English. Taken.
ANSWERS: 51; the ship's wheel looks ahead to "you who turn the wheel and look to windward" (320). The four stations are the medieval wheel's <i>regno, regnavi, sum sine regno, regnabo</i>: I reign, I have reigned, I have no kingdom, I shall reign.
