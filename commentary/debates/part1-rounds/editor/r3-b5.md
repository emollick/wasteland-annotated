# A fourth pass on nine pathway stops, prompted by the front-matter thread's own scholar and reader

The front-matter thread had a scholar and a reader read all 113 pathway stops before it learned the stops belonged to the part threads (their reports: commentary/debates/site/r1/F-scholar.md and F-reader.md). Their findings were on the stops as they stood before this debate's rewrite; most are already met. Nine stops still carried something they flagged: a reading stated as fact (dead@71, women@49, birds@23), a source named without a word of what it is (water@42, dead@62, dead@74, cities@60), a lake placed in a city (cities@8), and one knowing aside (cities@12). The editor's revisions:

### water@42
BODY: "Oed' und leer das Meer." Desolate and empty the sea: in the third act of Wagner's <i>Tristan</i>, the shepherd set to watch for Isolde's ship sees nothing on it.

### dead@62
BODY: Dante's crowd on London Bridge. Line 63 is from <i>Inferno</i> III, the train of the dead just inside the gate of Hell, as Eliot's note says: "I had not thought death had undone so many."

### dead@71
BODY: "That corpse you planted last year in your garden." Eliot's headnote promises readers of Frazer "references to vegetation ceremonies", and this is the plainest of them: the buried god, put in the ground so that he will come up again, here a corpse in a garden.

### dead@74
BODY: Cornelia's dirge in Webster's <i>The White Devil</i>, over "the friendless bodies of unburied men", with the wolf turned into a dog and the foe into a friend.

### women@49
BODY: Belladonna, the Lady of the Rocks. Belladonna is deadly nightshade, a poison and a cosmetic, and the word means beautiful lady; her title is usually traced to Leonardo's <i>Virgin of the Rocks</i>.

### cities@8
BODY: The Starnbergersee, the lake south-west of Munich, and the Hofgarten, in the city centre. Eliot spent the summer of 1911 in Munich.

### cities@12
BODY: Lithuania, in the poem's only German that is not quoted from Wagner: "I am not Russian at all, I come from Lithuania, real German."

### birds@23
BODY: "the cricket no relief." Not a bird. Eliot's cricket stands in for the grasshopper of Ecclesiastes 12:5, the verse his note cites, where "the grasshopper shall be a burden."

### cities@60
BODY: "Unreal City" is London. Eliot's note quotes Baudelaire on Paris, the "swarming city, city full of dreams, where the ghost in broad daylight accosts the passer-by."
