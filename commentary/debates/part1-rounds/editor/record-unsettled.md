# Points not settled, and the editor's changes after the last round

- **Where Lloyds Bank stood.** The batch 3 scholar wrote that the head office at 71 Lombard Street "faced the church across the street", and the batch 3 debate closed on that wording. The batch 5 scholar, checking the pathway stops, found only that the head offices filled the block between Lombard Street and Cornhill (71–77 Lombard Street, 15–22 Cornhill) and that Eliot's department is usually addressed 17 Cornhill, and would not vouch for which side of the street or which door. Lombard Street is 260 metres long and the church stands at its Bank end. The editor took the weaker claim in the notes on lines 63 and 67 and in the Eliot pathway stop at line 66: the head office "a few doors along from the church". Someone with a 1920s street directory or the bank's own history could make it exact.

- **Who calls at Myrtle's in the cancelled opening.** The drafts record and the gloss on line 1 follow Edmund Wilson's account of the facsimile (1971), which gives the call at Myrtle's to the narrator. The batch 4 scholar notes that the typescript introduces the passage with "Then we lost Steve" in parentheses, and that one reader has given it to Steve. Neither scholar could open the facsimile; the order of the night's events (Tom's place, the show, Myrtle's, the policeman on the way out, the man who smooths it over, the walk home at sunrise) is Wilson's and was checked against a second paraphrase of the draft.
- **The facsimile's own words on the cancellation.** The gloss on line 1 and the drafts record say the facsimile "attributes" the pencil stroke through the Boston opening to Eliot. Both scholars had it from Wilson's review and from accounts derived from the facsimile ("appears to have been" cancelled by Eliot); neither could open the book. If the facsimile hedges, "the stroke is taken to be Eliot's" is the wording to use, in both places.
- **Two changes the editor made after the last round.** The second way in ("Count the speakers") listed "a stranger in a café" and "a commuter"; after the front-matter thread renamed those voices in the legend, the editor changed the list to "a voice in German" and "a walker in the City", which is what the lens now shows. And the Boston drafts record was aligned with the gloss on line 1, which had settled the order of the night's events in batch 1.
- **Huxley and the date of the typescript.** The gloss on line 43 and the library proposal on <i>Crome Yellow</i> give both Eliot's 1952 letter (almost certain he took the name from the novel) and Lawrence Rainey's dating of the typescript to May 1921, before the novel was finished. The debate did not try to resolve it; the note carries both.
- **After the record was written.** The build thread pulled Part I at 19:18 UTC and changed three titles in ways.txt to match the site: the list heading drops its count ("Ways into The Burial of the Dead", the decision for all five parts); "Lay out the cards" is "Turn the tarot cards", after the tool it opens; and "Walk the commute" is "Follow the route in Part I", since the site no longer says the walk over London Bridge was Eliot's own way to work. It also answered the line-numbering point in section 5 of the proposals file: the README's sentence is about the printed marginal numbers and stands (a run-over line on page 42 of the 1922 book is not counted in the margin); Eliot's notes are the inconsistent party, 357 and 360 fitting the true count and 366 onward the printed one, which is why the site attaches note 366 to line 367.
- **The fourth pass on nine pathway stops (round four, applied 19:38 UTC).** The scholar verified all nine and corrected one: Cornelia sings the dirge in <i>The White Devil</i> over her murdered son, and "the friendless bodies of unburied men" are what the song asks the birds to cover. The reader gave plainer sentences for six; they were taken without a further scholar round, since they change no fact the scholar had checked (the one description they add, that the cricket is the first creature in the poem that sings, the old stop also carried). The Webster stop is the editor's merge of the scholar's correction and the reader's shape. The build thread was asked to pull Part I again before its deploy.
