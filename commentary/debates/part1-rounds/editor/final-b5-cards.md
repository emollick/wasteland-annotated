# Batch 5, the artist's card notes (art/tarot NOTES), as proposed after round three

### card sibyl
ANSWERS: The epigraph, from Petronius: the Sibyl of Cumae hangs in her jar while the boys ask "Sibyl, what do you want?" and she answers "I want to die." The bargain that brought her to this is told by Ovid: as many years as the grains of dust in her hand, and no youth to live them in.

### card phoenician-sailor
ANSWERS (last sentence only): He is drawn head down on purpose, in the posture of the Hanged Man on a tarot card, which is the card Madame Sosostris cannot find.

### card belladonna
ANSWERS: 49–50. "The Lady of the Rocks" is usually traced to Leonardo's <i>Virgin of the Rocks</i>; the drawing also remembers Walter Pater's Mona Lisa, who "is older than the rocks among which she sits." Belladonna is the plant, the poison, the cosmetic and "beautiful lady".

### card three-staves
ANSWERS: 51. Eliot's note ties him to the Fisher King, so his three staves are a crutch for the king's wound, a fishing rod with its line down, and a staff of office planted in the waste ground. The composition follows Pamela Colman Smith's Three of Wands in the Rider pack.

### card wheel
ANSWERS: 51; the ship's wheel looks ahead to "you who turn the wheel and look to windward" (320). The four stations are the medieval wheel's <i>regno, regnavi, sum sine regno, regnabo</i>: I reign, I have reigned, I have no kingdom, I shall reign.
