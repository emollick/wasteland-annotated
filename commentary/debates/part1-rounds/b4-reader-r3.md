"…a call at Myrtle's, where he is refused but given a bed, a bath and breakfast, and a walk home at sunrise." — the Myrtle item now has a list inside it, so at "a bath and breakfast, and a walk home" I read the walk as one more thing she gives him before I saw the outer list had resumed. Semicolons between the night's events fix it, keep every settled word, and imply no order. Replace the list with: "drinks at "Tom's place"; a show; a brush with a policeman that a man he knows smooths over; a call at Myrtle's, where he is refused but given a bed, a bath and breakfast; and a walk home at sunrise."

Everything else in the record passes: the cancellation sentence, the second-page sentence and the Pound sentence read as a person would say them, and nothing in it tells the reader what to feel.
