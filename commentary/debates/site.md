# Debate record: the site's own prose (front matter, notes, tools, pathways, library, listening room, tarot and map notes)

Thread: "Debate: front matter and notes". Run on 20 September 2026 by the thread's editor with two worker agents per batch, an Eliot scholar and a reader. This file is the record; the reports it summarises are in `debates/site/` (the brief, the batch lists, the round-one and round-two reports, and the editor's rewrites), and the final texts as applied are in `debates/site/final/`.

## What was debated

Everything in `commentary/site/` and the two art note files, in six batches:

- A. The site's own words: `pages.txt` (148 records: title page, page ledes, lens and tool names, About page, map and listening captions, colophon) and `ui.txt` (144 records: the lens cards at the head of the poem, the reading's nine tarot notes, the tools' text, card labels).
- B. The nine pathway introductions (`paths.txt`), the two drafts-page records (`drafts.txt`), the voices (`voices.json`), the timeline (`timeline.json`).
- C1 and C2. The library (`sources.txt`, 56 records, in two halves).
- D. The listening room (`listen.txt`, 26 records).
- E. The tarot pack's notes (`art/tarot/NOTES.md`, the 27 cards outside the reading and the back, which the site renders from that file) and the six map vignette titles (the `<title>` of each SVG, which is all the map shows).
- F. The 113 stop notes of the nine pathways, read from `data.js` and given a first round before it was found that the part threads had them in `part*/paths.txt` (see F below).

The commentary on Eliot's Notes as a whole is the `notes-head-text` and `notes-about` records in A; the recurring headings and labels are the lens, tool, page-title and card-kind records in A.

## The standard and the method

The standard is Ethan's message of 20 September and the brief written from it (`debates/site/BRIEF.md`): the plainness of a good scholarly editor; no tour-guide headings, no "X is a Y:" aphorisms with rhythmic lists, no clever counting, no bow-tying last sentences, no readings passed off as facts; a fact stated once, with a source where a reader would want it.

Round one: the scholar and the reader each read the whole batch and reported (`r1/`). The editor rewrote every record either side flagged, applying one rule throughout: the scholar's facts, the reader's wording (`rewrites/`). Round two: both re-read the rewrites and accepted or objected, with corrected text (`r2/`). The editor merged the round-two objections by the same rule and applied the result to the export files with a script that checks the file round-trips unchanged before it writes (`final/`). No record needed a third round; where the two sides still differed on a fact, the scholar's ruling stands and the point is listed under "Unsettled" below.

Every quotation from the poem and from Eliot's notes in the new text was checked word for word against the 1922 text (Gutenberg #1321, which is the Boni & Liveright printing; the Wikisource page scans for two disputed readings). Line numbers are the site's count.

## Unsettled

Points on which the two sides, or the sources, still differ; the text as applied takes the safer reading in each case.
- The typescript's length: printed accounts say 800 lines, about a thousand, and "almost twice"; nobody counted the facsimile. "Roughly twice its published length" is the hedge every account allows.
- Vivien Eliot's two lines (153 and 164): confirmed from secondary sources (tseliot.com, Mark Ford in the NYRB, Seymour-Jones), not against the facsimile; her exact pencilled words for 164 differ between sources, so the site says only that the lines are hers.
- Pound's dateline "24 Saturnus, An 1" or "An I": the sources split; the site quotes "24 Saturnus" only.
- The Margate departure, 12 November 1921: the site's date, not contradicted, not confirmed (Letters vol. 1 is the place to check).
- The inscription in Pound's copy: "il miglior fabbro" (Wikipedia) or "miglior fabbro" (the scholar's memory of Valerie Eliot's introduction and Ricks and McCue); the timeline keeps "il", which is safe if the first is right.
- The Pervigilium's date: modern editors favour the fourth century; the site says second to fifth.
- De Quadra's addressee: Froude and Eliot say Philip II; the Simancas calendar could not be reached; the record follows Froude.
- Whether the heading "He Do the Police in Different Voices" is struck through on the typescript: nothing now depends on it.
- The Woolf diary quotation (about-quote-1): three sentences from a diary first published in 1978; outside the brief's rights rule (not Eliot's words), kept as quotation for comment.

## For the art thread

Two of the tarot drawings say something the poem and the notes do not, and the notes now say so rather than passing it off as Eliot's: the Phoenician Sailor drawn head down as the Hanged Man; the merchant's blank card as Weston's Syrian burden. The Hanged Man card's inverted pose and bent leg are traditional to the card; only the halo (and the living tree) are Pamela Colman Smith's, and the note now says so. The Three of Wands composition follows Smith's card, as the note says.

From the Part I thread: the card crowd-on-london-bridge is drawn with three open arches and its note describes them; london-bridge-falling is drawn with five, and John Rennie's London Bridge of 1831, the one the crowd crossed in 1922, had five. Whichever the drawing settles on, the note should follow it.
