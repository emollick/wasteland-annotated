# Reader's report on the five "ways in" items (drafts/ways-replacements.txt), round 1

Read as READER-BRIEF.md asks: STANDARD.md, then the five items with the pathways they point at open beside them (merged/grail.txt; drafts/love.txt, drafts/method.txt, drafts/change.txt, drafts/passion.txt), poem.txt and eliot-notes.txt for every quotation, ways.txt for the form and length of the existing items, and the checkers' reports where they bear on a fact an item relies on (change line 315, passion line 391). Each item was tested as the brief tests a stop: plain on one reading; says what the pathway is for and why one would take it from this part; promises only what the pathway's stops deliver; free of the forbidden shapes; and its title, which is shown as a heading on the part's page, plain and true.

## Accepted as they stand

- The title of the Part I item, "Weston's plan, from the title on".
- The title of the Part II item, "The couples in the room and the pub".
- The title of the Part IV item, "The sea-change that does not come" (the Metamorphosis pathway's own stop at 315 opens "The poem's own drowned man has no sea-change", so the heading claims no more than the stop).

No item's body stands unchanged. The Part I body needs two words; the other four need recasting, for the reasons given below.

## The two facts asked in the instructions

**Brooks in the Part IV item.** The item gives Cleanth Brooks a reading "that the poem asks for a transformation it will not show". The Metamorphosis pathway gives him no such sentence. Its intro has his words "the way into life may be by death itself", and its stop at 315 has him take Phlebas for "the drowned god of the fertility cults"; the checker's report on that stop (rounds/change-check-r1.md) found Brooks's own sentences to be hedged ("The drowned Phoenician Sailor recalls the drowned god of the fertility cults"; "the way into life may be by death itself") and corrected the stop to those words. The corrected item below uses them and nothing beyond them.

**The cock in the Part V item.** "The cock that crowed for Peter" is not what the pathway's stop at 391 gives: there the cock is the bird of folk-lore "whose voice chases away the powers of evil" (Brooks) and the cock at whose crowing the ghost in <i>Hamlet</i> fades. Peter's cock is on the margin card at 392 ("Commentators compare the cock that crowed at Peter's denial (Matthew 26:74) and the cock of <i>Hamlet</i> I.i"), and the earlier reader's report on the pathway proposed only that the stop point to that card. The item should promise the cock, not Peter.

## Objections

### ways part 1 (grail)
Problem: "Readers of Frazer" is a surname dropped bare on a page that, once "Meet the dead" is retired, never says who Frazer was or what his book is about, and the sentence turns on knowing that (why would a reader of Frazer recognise a corpse in a garden?). Everything else holds: the headnote is quoted word for word, the five places named are the pathway's five stops in Part I, and "no sound of water" and "Fear death by water" match the poem.
Corrected:
title: Weston's plan, from the title on
body: Eliot's headnote credits Jessie L. Weston's book on the Grail legend with "not only the title, but the plan and a good deal of the incidental symbolism of the poem". The Grail pathway starts on the title page and stops at the places in Part I where the plan shows: the dead land and the dry stone with "no sound of water", the Tarot cards that Weston connected with the Grail, the warning "Fear death by water", and the corpse in Stetson's garden that readers of Frazer's <i>Golden Bough</i> were meant to recognise.

### ways part 2 (love)
Problem: The opening is a verbless fragment in three semicolon pieces, the shape the standard forbids (the old item had it too, which is why it is being replaced). "Stops three times in this section" is wrong: the pathway stops four times in Part II, at 77, 99, 111 and 139. "From the hyacinth garden to the thunder's "moment's surrender"" misplaces the end: the surrender at 404 is the speaker's answer to the thunder's "give", not the thunder's, and the pathway goes on to its last stop at 419, the heart that "would have responded" at 421, which its intro calls "the one love the poem speaks of as possible". Ophelia is a bare name. The title stands.
Corrected:
title: The couples in the room and the pub
body: In the room a woman asks to be spoken to and a man does not answer; in the pub Lil is told to get herself some teeth before Albert comes home, and the good night at closing time is Ophelia's, from <i>Hamlet</i>. The pathway called The loves that fail takes the poem's couples in order, from the hyacinth garden to the heart that "would have responded" at line 421, and stops four times in this section. Eliot's note on line 218 says "all the women are one woman"; the pathway lets you judge it.

### ways part 3 (method)
Problem: The title and "what the Thames now carries" say the opposite of the lines and of the pathway's stop at 176: the river "bears no" bottles, sandwich papers or cigarette ends, the nymphs "are departed", and the stop's point is that the device is "a list of what is not there". "Most of the pathway ... is in this section" is wrong (four stops of nine). "Eliot's 1923 sentences on the "mythical method"" quotes a phrase the pathway never uses; what the stop at 431 quotes is "a way of controlling, of ordering, of giving a shape and a significance", and the intro says the sentences were written about Joyce's <i>Ulysses</i>. "Marvell's chariot" and "Day's Actaeon" are names dropped without a word of what they are, and "Goldsmith's ruined woman" is plainer as the poem's own words.
Corrected:
title: Spenser's nymphs and the river they left
body: The pathway called The past beside the present stops four times in this section: Spenser's nymphs and the Thames they have left; Marvell's winged chariot and the rat; Actaeon coming to Diana in John Day's <i>Parliament of Bees</i> and Sweeney to Mrs. Porter; Goldsmith's song of a woman who "stoops to folly" and the gramophone. Take them in order and see what each pairing does. The pathway begins with Chaucer's April and ends at "These fragments I have shored against my ruins", with Eliot's 1923 sentences on the method of Joyce's <i>Ulysses</i> beside it.

### ways part 4 (change)
Problem: The last sentence gives Cleanth Brooks a reading the pathway does not give, "that the poem asks for a transformation it will not show"; the pathway has only his verified words, "the way into life may be by death itself" (intro) and the drowned god of the fertility cults (stop 315, which the checker corrected to Brooks's own "recalls"). "Is set out there and marked as his" is the site's working language showing through. "Ariel's song" needs <i>The Tempest</i>, and Philomel and Tiresias, bare in a list, need a word each on a page that does not otherwise explain them. The first two sentences and the title stand. (If the merge keeps the draft stop's "took Phlebas for the drowned god", the item's "recalls" is still true to Brooks as the checker quoted him.)
Corrected:
title: The sea-change that does not come
body: Ariel's song in <i>The Tempest</i>, which the poem quotes at 48 and 125, promises that the drowned king's body will suffer "a sea-change / Into something rich and strange". Phlebas gets a current that picks his bones. The Metamorphosis pathway follows the poem's changed bodies, Philomel's (a nightingale), Tiresias's (a woman for seven years) and the drowned man's, and reaches this section at line 315. There the pathway quotes Cleanth Brooks (1939), for whom the drowned Phoenician Sailor recalls the drowned god of the fertility cults and "the way into life may be by death itself".

### ways part 5 (passion)
Problem: Three things promised are not what the stops give. "The trial" is not in the stop at 322, which gives the arrest by night (John 18:3), Gethsemane (Luke 22:44) and the death; "the women's lamentation" states as a fact what the stop at 367 gives as Hugh Kenner's reading, against Eliot's own note, which gives Hesse; and "the cock that crowed for Peter" is on the margin card, not in the stop at 391 (see above). "The arrest, the trial and the death" is also a list of three for rhythm. "Ends where Eliot's note on the last line does" is a turn the reader has to work out; the stop at 434 says plainly that the note glosses the word with the blessing that ends the Communion service. The title's comma holds back the word for effect; the pathway's own phrase, "the Passion without its names", is plainer and says the same.
Corrected:
title: The Passion without its names
body: Lines 322 to 330 tell the arrest by night, the agony in the garden and the death of Christ without naming him, and the section goes on to the road to Emmaus, a "maternal lamentation" heard high in the air, Ezekiel's dry bones, the cock on the rooftree and Isaiah's warning to a dying king. The pathway called The Bible and the Prayer Book takes them in order, and ends at the last line, where Eliot's note glosses "Shantih" with words from the Prayer Book's blessing at the end of Holy Communion.

Accepted 0 of the 5 items as they stand (the titles of Parts I, II and IV stand); 5 objections (one per item: Part I two words, Parts II to V recast, with the Part III and Part V titles changed).
