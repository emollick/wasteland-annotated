## Disagreements decided for the scholar

1. line 12: the reader's "[to check]" on 1918 against 1919 settled by giving each date its owner (the card's 1918, Southam's "only from 1919"), as the checker proposed.
2. line 12: "Hugh Kenner (1959) heard in Marie's voice" (reader) made "in the voice of these lines": Kenner's sentence begins "Another voice evokes" and does not name her.
3. line 70: "Eliot's friend John Hayward" (reader, from the draft) replaced by the checker's description of him, which is what Southam's introduction says and what gives the quotation its weight for this pathway.
4. line 70: "as every man" put in quotation marks as Brooks's own phrase, which the checker found on the page.
5. line 115: the words the line answers named as the cards give them, "What are you thinking of?" (the reader's text had dropped the draft's "Stay with me" without naming the right ones).
6. line 139: "closing by ten at night" (reader) replaced by "ending not later than ten at night": the Act's hours are a limit, not a fixed closing time; and "then fixed by", since the Act came into force after the passage was written.
7. line 369: "Southam's note on the hordes connects them with the Bolshevik Revolution of 1917" (reader, from the draft) replaced: Southam refers the hordes to Hesse, and his Revolution is a gloss on the "Murmur of maternal lamentation" of 368.
8. line 369: "the five cities of 375–376, among them Vienna" (reader) replaced by Brooks's four, named; London is his comparison, not one of the counted.
9. line 369: "the close of an essay in Hermann Hesse's Blick ins Chaos" (reader) made the first of the three essays, with its subject, as the checker's text and the card have it.
10. line 427: "Southam's note on the line prints its first verse" (reader) made "prints its refrain", with the three lines quoted; "his note on line 62 allows an echo of it already in the crowd flowing over London Bridge" (reader, from the draft) replaced by Kenner's link, the Southam-on-62 citation dropped.
