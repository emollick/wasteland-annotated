## What a pathway is

A sequence of stops in the order of the poem. The reader chooses a pathway and the poem page opens with a small guide in the corner that moves from stop to stop; at each stop the poem is scrolled to the line and the guide shows a short note (one to four sentences). Line numbers are the site's (STANDARD.md); line 0 is the title page (epigraph, dedication). A pathway has a title, a one-sentence reason, and an intro paragraph, shown where the pathways are offered. The marginal cards already on the poem page (glosses-index.txt) give sources line by line; a pathway is different: it follows one theme through the whole poem and says at each stop what the passage does for that theme and why it matters. A stop should not repeat a card's job (giving the source); it should say what the theme makes of the passage, and may point to the card or the library.

## Your task

Design the set. Decide the pathways, their order, their titles, reasons and intros (draft), and for each pathway the stops, in line order, with one or two sentences saying what each stop will say and which sources the stop will cite. Do not write the finished stop notes; other scholars will write them from your design, one pathway each, so your design must give them enough to work from: the point of the stop, the reading or fact it rests on, the source.

Constraints:
- Between eight and twelve thematic pathways, each of eight to fourteen stops, in line order, spanning as much of the poem as the theme honestly allows (a pathway need not visit every part).
- A stop is one line number (the first line of the passage it is about). Two pathways may stop at the same line when the theme demands it, but avoid making the set repetitive: where two pathways would say the same thing at a line, decide which one owns it and let the other say something else or pass. Note the overlaps you decided.
- Ids: a single lowercase word not already in use (in use: first, water, dead, women, cities, birds, nothing, eyes, eliot). Suggested, change if you have better: grail, passion, east (or buddha), dante, love (or tiresias), war, wagner, method, seers.
- A candidate set is below as a starting point. Accept, change, merge, split, drop or add, but every pathway must be one the poem and its scholarship actually carry: name, for each, the scholarship behind it (the commentaries, the essays, Eliot's own notes), and be ready to defend it against a reader who asks "is this really in the poem?".
- Say where the matter of the retired pathways goes (Water, The dead, The women, Birds, Nothing, Eyes): each retired pathway's real content should be absorbed somewhere, or you should say why it need not be.
- The five "ways in" items that point at retired pathways (ways.txt: Part I "Meet the dead", Part II "The women, one after another", Part III "Every bird", Part IV "The drowned before Phlebas", Part V "Every 'nothing'") need replacements pointing at new pathways: propose which new pathway each should point at, with a draft title and body in the same register.
