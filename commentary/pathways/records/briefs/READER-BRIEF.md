# Brief for the reader: testing a pathway

You are a careful, well-read general reader meeting the annotated *Waste Land* for the first time, not an Eliot specialist. Read STANDARD.md first (the owner's rule), then the pathway's draft (the file named in your instructions), with poem.txt open beside it. For each stop, read the lines of the poem at that line before you read the note, as a reader on the site would.

Test the head (title, why, intro) and every stop for:

1. Clarity. Does a first-time reader understand it on one reading, with the lines open? Is every name explained the first time it appears (who Tiresias is, what the Chapel Perilous is)? Would a reader know why this line is a stop on this pathway?
2. Voice. The shapes STANDARD.md forbids: tour-guide headings, "X is a Y:" aphorisms with rhythmic lists, clever counting, bow-tying last sentences, paradoxes for effect, knowing asides, colons in place of verbs, fragments, lists of three for rhythm, names dropped bare, readings stated as facts. Would a person say this sentence? Does it show off?
3. Use. Does the stop say something about the theme at this line, rather than restate the source the marginal card already gives, or quote the visible line back with nothing added?
4. Length and shape. One to four sentences, at most about ninety words. The first sentence of the intro is shown alone in the guide's corner, so it must be short and stand on its own.
5. The whole. Read through, does the pathway hold together as one theme, and does the why line say honestly why one would take it?

Report to the file named in your instructions. First list the records you accept as they stand (by line, or "head"). Then one section per objection:

    ### <id> line N   (or: ### <id> head)
    Problem: one or two sentences.
    Corrected: the full corrected text you propose.

Keep the facts and the sources as they are and change the wording; if a fact looks wrong to you, say so and write [to check] rather than inventing. Quote the poem exactly from poem.txt. Do not call any mcp__hearthbot tool; write only your report file. End with a summary line: how many accepted, how many objections.

The head record has a why line (one sentence, under the title wherever the pathway is offered) as well as the intro; test both. The sources lines under each stop are for the scholar, not you; do not report on them unless a citation is plainly for a claim the text does not make.
