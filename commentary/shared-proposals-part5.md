# Proposals from the Part V debate for text outside part5/

From the thread "Debate: What the Thunder Said", 20 September 2026, after three rounds between an Eliot scholar and a reader; the reasoning is in debates/part5.md under the handles named here. These are the sentences Part V opens that live in site/ (pathway intros, library records, voice notes, card notes) and in the page templates (the Water lens intro and the map title, Ethan's two examples). Take or leave each; the Part V files do not depend on them, except that the voice note on the Fisher King now contradicts the Part V way on voices (item 6).


## 1. The Water lens intro (template prose; Ethan's own example)
Now: "Every line that is wet, dry or burning, tinted. The poem's argument is a weather report: rain that is unwelcome, rivers that are dirty, a sea that drowns, and then twenty-nine lines of rock before three words of rain."
Proposed: "Tints every line that is wet, dry or burning. The last rain the poem shows falling is the shower of line 9; the gust of line 395 brings rain the leaves are still waiting for."
Why: the "X is a Y:" opening and the list of three; "three words" is wrong ("Bringing rain" is two); the rain at 395 is not shown falling.

## 2. The map page title and the Places lens description ("Where the poem is standing"; Ethan's other example)
Proposed: "The poem's places" for the map page; "Places" or "The places the poem names" for the lens description.

## 3. Pathway intros (site/paths.txt)
- first: "Twelve stops for a first reading. Each gives a line or two and a short note."
- water: "Every line in which water is present or wanted, from the spring rain of line 4 to the gust of line 395." (The old intro's "Follow the water and you follow the argument:" is the twin of the weather-report sentence; "to the last rain" is wrong, since the last rain shown falling is line 9.)
- dead: "Burials, drownings, bones and the crowd that death has undone. The poem's first section is called The Burial of the Dead, from the Book of Common Prayer; the dead appear in every part."
- women: keep the first two sentences; cut "Most of them speak; several are quotations; two are queens; one is a bird."
- cities: "The poem's geography, in the order the poem gives it. It begins in Munich and ends in Aquitaine, with London at its centre." ("ends on the Ganges" is wrong: London Bridge and Aquitaine follow; "five capitals" should be "five cities".)
- birds: "Every bird named in the poem, in order. Recordings, where they exist, are in the listening room." ("every song is a complaint" is untrue of the hermit-thrush and the cock.)
- eyes: keep the first sentence; cut "The poem's people cannot see, or see too much."
- eliot: "Places and dates from Eliot's own life in 1921 and 1922 that appear in the poem." (The old intro ends on "a map of where he was standing".)

## 4. Library records (site/sources.txt), Part V sources
- weston: replace "It does not matter." with "the poem used the book anyway."; replace the last sentence ("Read chapters II ... a very good bad book") with "The chapters the poem draws on most are II (The Task of the Hero), IX (The Fisher King) and XIII (The Perilous Chapel)."
- dante-inf33: trans and note should allow "locked" as well as "nailed up" for "chiavar"; end the note "Eliot's note pairs the passage with Bradley on the privacy of experience; see the note on line 412." instead of "everyone in a tower, listening for the key."
- whitman-lilacs: replace the last two sentences with: "Eliot's notes do not mention Whitman. He reviewed a life of Whitman in 1926, and wrote in 1928 that he 'did not read Whitman until much later in life'; the connection is the commentators'."
- ezekiel: replace the last two sentences with "In Ezekiel the bones live again; in the poem, 'Dry bones can harm no one.'"
- luke24: cut the last sentence (Caravaggio).
- ovid-met6: replace "The story sits above the mantel ... as a swallow." with "The story is in the poem at lines 99, 203 and 429."; "is the English to go to" becomes "is the English to read."
- brihadaranyaka: cut "The fable is about hearing what you need to hear in a single syllable."; "the notes end by calling the English 'a feeble translation'" should add "in 1922; from 1925 the note reads 'our equivalent'."
- bradley: replace the last two sentences with "Eliot's note sets the page beside Ugolino's tower, in the lines that answer the thunder's 'Dayadhvam,' sympathise."
- shackleton-south: cut the last sentence (Hurley's photographs).
- hesse-blick-ins-chaos: add after the first sentence of the note: "Stephen Hudson's English version, <i>In Sight of Chaos</i> (1923), has the two Dostoevsky essays." (Moved here from the Part V gloss, where it was bibliography rather than help with the line.)
- chapman-birds: replace "Listen to a recording: ... really does sound like water dripping into a pool. It sings at dusk, and the poem's 'Drip drop ...' is a fair transcription." with "The phrase 'water-dripping song' in Eliot's note is not Chapman's, and no source for it is agreed. A recording is in the listening room." Keep the 1902 gift (confirmed: his fourteenth birthday).
- pervigilium-veneris: cut the last sentence ("The whole of the poem's spring ...").
- nerval-desdichado: replace the last two sentences with "Eliot quotes the second line only."
- kyd-spanish-tragedy: cut the last sentence.
- london-bridge: name Alice Gomme (Traditional Games, 1894–98) for the foundation-sacrifice reading; cut "and the only one in English a child would know."
- tennyson: "The speaker of <i>Maud</i> imagines himself buried under a city street with the traffic going over him; compare 'rats' alley' in Part II. The swallow song in <i>The Princess</i> gives Eliot the English half of a Latin line. He called Tennyson's 'the finest ear of any English poet since Milton' (1936)."

## 5. Card notes (site/ui.txt), Part V cards
- the-third: cut "No accent: the plainest card after the blank." (a production note).
- london-bridge-falling: cut "No accent. Not to be confused with crowd-on-london-bridge.svg (60)." (a production note with a filename).
- swallow: the note quotes "Quando fiam uti chelidon"; the site's 1922 text reads "ceu". Quote the site's line.

## 6. Voice notes (site/voices.json), Part V speakers
- poem: now "The voice that opens the poem, describes the room in Part II, elegises Phlebas, and speaks the thunder's glosses. It is never quite a person." Proposed: "The poem's own voice, not a character: it opens the poem, describes the room in Part II, speaks the elegy for Phlebas, and answers the thunder's three words." (The old note ends on an aphorism, and "glosses" is the site's word for its own notes.)
- pilgrim (label "The pilgrim on the road"): note is plain; keep. The label decides among Eliot's three themes; "The walker on the road" would not. For the site thread to weigh.
- seer (label "The one who sees the cities fall"): now "Lines 367–395: the hooded hordes, the falling towers, the nightmare, the empty chapel." Proposed: "Lines 367 to 395: the hooded hordes, the falling towers, the woman and the bats, the empty chapel." ("the nightmare" tells the reader how to feel about lines 378 to 385.)
- thunder: now "DA. Three times." Proposed: "The thunder's one syllable, at lines 401, 411 and 418."
- fisherking: now "Lines 424–426, and the fragments that follow: 'I sat upon the shore.'" Proposed: "Lines 424 to 426: 'I sat upon the shore / Fishing, with the arid plain behind me.'" (The lens gives lines 427 to 432 to the fragments, not to the Fisher King; the site's own data says so, and the Part V way on voices now describes it correctly.)
- fragments: now "Lines 427–432: a nursery rhyme, Dante, a Latin poet, Nerval, the poet, Kyd, in that order." Proposed: "Lines 427 to 432: a nursery rhyme, Dante, a Latin poet and Tennyson, Nerval, the poet, Kyd's Hieronimo, in that order." (Line 429 is half Tennyson; the Tongues way and the fragments gloss say so.)
- upanishad: now "Datta. Dayadhvam. Damyata. Shantih shantih shantih." Proposed: "Lines 433 and 434: the thunder's three words repeated, and the word that ends an Upanishad."
