# The commentary after the debates

20 September 2026. Written by the build thread for Ethan, after the six debates were pulled back into the site. The site is at https://the-waste-land.netlify.app.

## How the debates were run

At 17:28 you said the commentary read in the voice of an LLM and asked for Eliot-scholar and reader agents to debate every section. Six debates ran between 17:30 and 19:20, one for each part of the poem and one for everything else the site says in its own words: the page titles and ledes, the lens and tool names, the library, the listening room, the voices, the timeline, the pathway introductions, the tarot notes and the map. Each had an editor and two reviewers who read every note independently. The scholar was briefed on the 1971 facsimile, Ricks and McCue, Southam, Rainey, Grover Smith, Kenner, Gardner, Gordon and Crawford, and on the source texts, with the web to check them. The reader was briefed to ask of each note whether it is clear, whether it helps with the line, whether a person would say it, and whether it shows off. The editor rewrote what either side flagged and sent the rewrite back; two rounds were usual, three where the sides still differed. On substance the scholar had the last word, on wording the reader. Every quotation from the poem and from Eliot's notes in the new text was checked against the 1922 Boni and Liveright printing.

The full records are in this folder under `debates/`: every note as it stood, each side's report in each round, the editor's decisions, and the points left unsettled. The shorter `before-and-after` files beside them show the old and new text side by side.

## What changed

Counted by records whose text or title changed.

- Part I, The Burial of the Dead: all 24 notes, all ten ways in, 29 of the 38 pathway stops, the drafts record.
- Part II, A Game of Chess: all ten notes, the ways in, all 18 stops, the drafts record.
- Part III, The Fire Sermon: all 19 notes, the eleven ways in, 21 of the 28 stops, the three drafts records.
- Part IV, Death by Water: both notes, the six ways in, all five stops, the drafts record.
- Part V, What the Thunder Said: all 13 notes, the twelve ways in, 17 of the 23 stops, both drafts records.
- The site's own words: 49 of the 148 page texts and 41 of the 144 tool texts; all nine pathway introductions; the two drafts-page records; 25 of the 33 voice notes; 16 of the 26 timeline entries; most of the 56 library records; 24 of the 26 listening-room records; the notes on the 27 tarot cards outside the reading; the six map vignette titles.

The two examples you gave:

- "Where the poem is standing" is now "The map", and the Places lens reads "The places the poem names, and the map".
- The Water lens read "Every line that is wet, dry or burning, tinted. The poem's argument is a weather report: rain that is unwelcome, rivers that are dirty, a sea that drowns, and then twenty-nine lines of rock before three words of rain." It now reads "Lines that mention water (rain, river, sea), drought (rock, stone, dust) or fire are tinted. The bar below shows how much of the poem each takes, and the count under it how many lines."

## Names and headings

Names that told a first-time reader nothing were changed. The Echoes lens is Sources and Tongues is Languages; the "Do" button is Tools, and its seven items say what happens: Read line by line, Turn the tarot cards, Unstack the last lines, Draw a line at random, Find a word, Learn it by heart, Take a pathway. Page titles the same: "What Pound cut" is "The drafts", "The 434th line called 433" is "The line numbering", "Madame Sosostris deals" is "The tarot cards", "The fragments, unstacked" is "The last eight lines", "Voices from 1922" is "Four remarks on the poem". The "ways in" headings lost their counts. The button that was "Walk the commute" is "Follow the route in Part I", because the claim that the London Bridge route was Eliot's daily walk to work could not be established and is gone from the site; the route is now the way City workers came in from London Bridge station.

## Corrections of fact

The scholars checked every quotation and reference. Among the corrections:

- The Sibyl hangs in a jar (Petronius's ampulla), not a bottle.
- Lombard Street is not named in the poem; the stops and the timeline no longer say it is.
- Vivien Eliot wrote two lines of Part II (153 and 164), not one.
- Woolf's diary entry on Eliot's reading is 23 June 1922. Pound's "Complimenti" letter is 24 January 1922, which Pound dated "24 Saturnus".
- Augustine's cauldron is in Book III of the Confessions and the "beautiful things" passage in Book X.
- Eliot's "By the waters of Leman" follows the Prayer Book wording of Psalm 137, not the King James.
- In Ovid both sisters kill Itys, and Tereus becomes a hoopoe; the library record now has the passage and Miller's 1916 translation.
- "Dans le Restaurant" was written in 1917, printed in September 1918 and collected in 1920; its translation now agrees between the library and the Part IV note.
- The Jeremy Irons recording in the listening room plays the Radio 4 programme of 1 January 2017, in which he reads alone; the record now says so.
- Six of the nine pathway introductions misdescribed the stops the pathway makes; all nine are now checked against the stops.
- Eliot's words from outside the 1922 text and notes (the "rhythmical grumbling" remark, the 1923 letter to Russell, "The Frontiers of Criticism") are cut to a phrase each, as the rights rule asks.

One reading the debates raised was not followed. The site prints Eliot's note on the thunder as "(Give, sympathise, control)", which is what the 1922 Boni and Liveright pages show; the Gutenberg transcription has "sympathize", and the site thread had changed its quotations to match it. They were changed back to the printed spelling.

## What the debates left open

Each record ends with the points the two sides did not settle, and the text takes the safer reading in each case: dates the printed accounts give differently (the length of the typescript, Pound's dateline, the day the Eliots left Margate), readings now marked as readings rather than facts (the Sailor drawn as the Hanged Man, the second speaker at line 12), and details nobody could check without the facsimile or the recording (how far the extracts in the listening room run). They are listed under "Unsettled" in `debates/site.md` and at the end of each part's record.

## Landed since the account was written

- The Part I debate's notes on the shared files are in: 18 library records, both drafts-page records, two tarot notes (the one-eyed merchant, the Hanged Man) and three voice notes. Mr. Eugenides no longer has a voice of his own in the Voices lens: his invitation is reported, so lines 207 to 214 belong to the walker.
- The tarot card of the crowd on London Bridge now shows the five arches of Rennie's bridge of 1831, the one Eliot crossed, as the note beside it says.
- Six pathway stops in Parts II, III and V take corrections the site debate found after the part debates had closed: "rats' alley" (115) gives the trenches as a reading and Eliot's note's rat at 195 as his own pointer; the stops at 182 say Parts IV and V were written at Lausanne, not that the poem was finished there; Mrs Porter's ballad (199) is quoted from Eliot's note and the Australian troops' song given as an identification; the hermit-thrush stop (357) links the three recordings in the listening room; the stop at 396 loses "the monsoon", which the poem does not have.
- Nine Part I pathway stops were revised in a fourth pass by its debate thread after the site debate's readers had gone through every stop: a source named with a word of what it is (Cornelia's dirge in <i>The White Devil</i>, sung over her murdered son; <i>Inferno</i> III for the crowd on the bridge), the Starnbergersee placed outside Munich rather than in it, and the readings marked as readings.

## One question

At most stops of the Nothing and Eyes pathways, and at some of the Dead and Cities stops, the box shows the line itself and nothing more. It is left that way, as a highlight. If each stop should say something, the reader's report has a sentence for each, and they can be put through the same review.
