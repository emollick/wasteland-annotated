line: 55
text: "Fear death by water." Madame Sosostris warns against water. In the Grail legend that Eliot's headnote points to, water is what the waste land lacks and needs, and Cleanth Brooks (1939) read her warning as a warning against the one death that might bring life. Part IV of the poem is called Death by Water.

path: eliot
line: 0
text: Paris, January 1922. Eliot stopped there on his way back from Lausanne, and Pound went through the typescript with him, cutting it hard. The dedication came later. In January 1923 Eliot inscribed Pound's copy to him as "miglior fabbro", and from 1925 the dedication was printed with the poem. The words are Dante's, for the poet Arnaut Daniel, and mean the better craftsman.
sources: Dante, Purgatorio XXVI 117
Poems 1909–1925
Facsimile (1971), the typescripts with Pound's annotations
King's College, Cambridge, "The Waste Land at 100"
Rainey, Revisiting The Waste Land, ch. 1
